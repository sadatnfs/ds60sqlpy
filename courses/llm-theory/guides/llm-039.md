## Purpose and mental model

<!-- textbook-advanced-start -->

### Make a finite safety result proportional to its evidence

A test suite observes a finite set of cases under a particular system configuration. It can reveal failures, estimate rates under stated sampling assumptions and check required behaviors. It cannot enumerate every possible future input or prove an unrestricted zero-risk claim. The purpose of careful reporting is not to make every result sound uncertain; it is to distinguish what was observed from what the design can reasonably support.

The worksheet uses fictional groups and authored cases. Group A has zero failures in ten cases, while group B has zero failures in one case. Both observed rates are zero. They do not carry the same amount of information, and neither observation makes a population failure probability exactly zero. Keeping the numerator and denominator visible is the first step toward a defensible interpretation.

#### Derive what zero observed failures can mean

Suppose, for a deliberately simplified calculation, that N cases are independent draws from a stable population with the same unknown failure probability p. The probability of observing zero failures is \((1-p)^N\). Setting that probability equal to a tail level alpha gives an upper boundary \(p_{\mathrm{upper}}=1-{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{N}}\) for the corresponding exact one-sided binomial construction when zero failures were observed.

At \(\alpha=0.05\), \(N=1\) gives \(p_{\mathrm{upper}}=0.95\). For \(N=10\), the value is about .2589. For \(N=100\), it is about .0295. These are not claims that the actual risk equals those boundaries. They illustrate how much a zero count can still leave unresolved under the stated independent, identically distributed sampling model. The familiar approximation \(\frac{3}{N}\) comes from \(-\frac{\log0.05}{N}\) for sufficiently large N, not from a universal rule that every zero-failure test proves risk below that number.

The assumptions matter as much as the formula. Ten paraphrases of one easy question are not necessarily ten independent draws from the intended deployment population. A hand-selected adversarial test set may be excellent for finding a specific weakness while not estimating an ordinary-traffic failure rate. A population that changes after deployment violates the stable-distribution assumption. Report the case-selection process instead of attaching a binomial interval to any convenient count.

#### Separate group rates from group evidence

If both groups show zero failures, saying "the groups perform equally" confuses equal observed rates with equal underlying behavior. Group B's single case leaves much more uncertainty and may not cover its important subtypes. Increasing the number of independent cases in B could reveal a failure pattern that was invisible in the initial sample. A denominator is part of the evidence, not a footnote to the percentage.

Group definitions should match the task and evaluation purpose. Fictional A/B labels in this lesson teach arithmetic without collecting personal data. In a real study, categories can be incomplete, overlapping or measured with error, and the relevant fairness criterion depends on the decision being made. A parity statistic should not be chosen solely because it is easy to compute or happens to produce a favorable number.

Aggregates can also hide within-group structure. A high overall rate can coexist with a poor result on a rare task family or intersection of conditions. Inspect meaningful slices with their counts and acknowledge when a slice has too little evidence for a stable estimate. Splitting a small dataset into many tiny groups does not create precision; it reveals where more data or a narrower claim is needed.

#### A counterfactual pair needs a justified expected relation

A counterfactual test changes one aspect of an input while attempting to preserve the task-relevant facts. If the answer should remain the same, a changed response can expose inconsistent treatment. But invariance is not automatically correct for every edit. Changing an attribute that legitimately affects the requested decision can require a different answer.

For example, a fictional eligibility question might depend on a stated age threshold. Changing the age across that threshold should change the answer; changing an irrelevant name while keeping all eligibility facts fixed should not. A test that demands invariance under both edits would contain a flawed oracle. Before interpreting a model difference as unfairness or robustness failure, explain why the chosen transformation should preserve or alter the expected result.

Pairs should be evaluated under the same model, evidence and scoring policy. A difference caused by a retrieved document changing between runs is not isolated to the textual counterfactual. Stochastic generation can add another source of variation. Preserve the actual paired outputs and conditions, and use a design appropriate to the claim rather than assuming a single differing sample proves a stable causal effect.

#### Severe failures need explicit decision rules

The second worked case has nine passes and one severe failure. The overall pass rate is 90 percent, but a predeclared requirement of zero observed severe failures is violated. Averaging the severe case with nine routine successes would change the decision policy. A required condition can block release even when the overall mean looks favorable.

This does not mean every failure should receive an identical severity label. The rubric must define the consequence or contract violation being assessed, and scorers need enough context to apply it consistently. Record the case, observed behavior, severity rationale and whether the outcome is reproducible. Unknown severity or incomplete review should remain explicit rather than being silently converted to a pass.

A useful gate is a statement about evidence required for a particular use. It is not a universal certificate of safety. Passing the gate on a fixed test set supports a bounded decision under that policy; it does not eliminate the need to handle failures outside the tested cases. Conversely, a no-go can be a successful evaluation outcome when it identifies the missing or failed condition precisely.

#### Robustness testing changes inputs in controlled ways

Robustness concerns how behavior changes under relevant perturbations: paraphrases, spelling variation, reordered context, missing evidence, contradictory sources or other task-specific changes. A perturbation should have a defined relationship to the intended answer. If the edit changes the meaning, a changed output may be correct rather than a failure.

Distinguish natural variation from adversarial stress cases. Natural variation can help estimate behavior across a workload distribution if sampled appropriately. Stress cases can expose boundary failures even when their frequency is unknown. Both are useful, but their denominators support different claims. Do not report the pass rate on a hand-constructed stress set as though it were a measured production incident rate.

Repeatedly repairing a system against the same stress cases can overfit the test set. Preserve fresh cases or families for later assessment, and document which failures influenced prompt, model or tool-policy changes. A test suite's usefulness as a development aid does not guarantee that it remains an untouched final evaluation.

#### Build a result that remains useful after a failure

A strong report includes the system/configuration identity, task scope, case-selection method, group and family counts, observed failures, severity rubric, required gates and unresolved conditions. It distinguishes a mechanism check from an empirical rate and a policy decision. A statement such as "\(\frac{0}{10}\) observed under these authored cases" is more informative than "safe" because another reader can see both the observation and its boundary.

For a subject-matter assistant, include unsupported-answer behavior and tool-boundary violations alongside ordinary answer accuracy. A model can answer many questions correctly while mishandling a missing-evidence case or attempting an unavailable operation. The preceding chapters supplied those mechanism-specific records; this chapter explains how to retain them without letting an aggregate erase their significance.

## Vocabulary and concepts

**Counterfactual pair:** Two cases differing in a controlled attribute.

**Severity:** The consequence category of a failure, separate from frequency.

**Finite evidence:** Observations from a bounded set of tested cases.

## Zero observed failures is a finite observation

Group A has zero failures in ten trials; group B has zero failures in one trial. Both observed rates are zero, but the amount of evidence is very different. Under an explicit independent, identically distributed Bernoulli model with failure probability p, the probability of seeing zero failures in n trials is \({\left(1-p\right)}^{n}\). Setting this probability to 0.05 gives the one-sided 95% zero-failure upper confidence bound \(p_{\mathrm{upper}}=1-{0.05}^{\frac{1}{n}}\).

For \(n=10\) the bound is about 0.2589; for \(n=1\) it is 0.95. These large values show how little zero failures can establish with very small samples. The formula depends on the sampling model. Ten paraphrases from one prompt family may not behave like ten independent trials, and a hand-selected challenge set does not automatically estimate deployment prevalence. State the unit and collection process before attaching a population interpretation to an interval.

An untested group has no observed rate. Filling it with zero would confuse absence of evidence with observed success. Group definitions also need a task-relevant rationale and enough coverage to support the intended comparison. A pooled result can hide a concentrated failure in one slice, while many tiny slices can produce unstable estimates. Preserve counts and concrete failure examples alongside any aggregate.

## Robustness tests need a justified invariance

A counterfactual pair changes a feature while holding the task-relevant information constant. If the changed attribute should not affect the answer under the task specification, a different answer is a useful diagnostic. Not every attribute change should preserve output: changing an actual causal input to the requested calculation may correctly change the result. Specify why invariance is expected before grading the pair.

A severe-failure gate can remain unsatisfied despite 90% overall success. If the predeclared policy disallows a particular severe failure, averaging it with many easy passes does not remove it. Conversely, a passing finite challenge set cannot establish that all future cases are safe. The worksheet supplies fictional cases and a reasoning framework, not personal-data analysis or a deployment approval. A defensible report states the tested scenarios, counts, collection method, observed failures and unresolved coverage.


## Worked example / walkthrough

### Example A: Read group denominators

Equal observed rates do not imply equal uncertainty. The denominator is a central part of the result and should remain visible in a report.

```text
A: failures0,count10,observed rate0/10
B: failures0,count1,observed rate0/1
Neither establishes a zero population failure probability.
```

**Expected:** both observed zero;B has much less evidence.

### Example B: Expose a severe failure

Averages cannot override an explicitly required critical condition. The decision rule should be established before seeing which case failed.

```text
Pass rate = 9/10 = 90percent
Severe failures = 1
Required severe-failure gate = fail
```

**Expected:** 90percent passes;gate fails.

## Common pitfalls and diagnosis

Keep zero observed failures, unknown coverage and a guaranteed zero population rate separate. State the independence and sampling assumptions behind an interval. Explain why a counterfactual should preserve the task answer, and apply severe-failure gates without averaging them away in an overall pass rate.
