## The question and the mechanism

Choose an aggregation whose input grain and total behavior match the metric definition.

A sum over disjoint sets is additive when each event contributes once in a common unit. Distinct counts are not generally additive because the same identity can appear in more than one group. The difference is semantic, not a limitation of a visual. A grand total of distinct orders should ask for distinct orders in the entire context, not blindly add the row-level counts.

Our retail fact has eight lines, seven orders and three customers with sales. The Product dimension has four catalog members, including an unsold bag. These populations can all be useful, but they answer different questions. Name the denominator or counted entity explicitly so downstream ratios do not switch populations unnoticed.

A measure is reusable only when its definition is stable. If 'active customer' means at least one eligible sale, define that eligibility before counting. If it means enrolled customers regardless of sales, the dimension or an enrollment fact is the correct starting point. A formula that happens to match the expected total on this fixture may still fail when a multi-product order or unsold customer appears.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

The same four aggregate expressions are evaluated under all rows and under Paper. The Product filter travels to Sales, so fact-based distinct counts change with that selection. Customers can buy products from several categories, which is why category-level customer counts cannot be added to produce a unique customer total.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **SUM**, **COUNTROWS**, **DISTINCTCOUNT**, **additivity**, **population**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
    MEASURE Sales[OrderCount] = DISTINCTCOUNT ( Sales[OrderID] )
```

### Case A: Whole-model populations

**Starting context:** All rows; complete nonblank order/customer fact keys.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "Revenue", [NetRevenue], "Lines", COUNTROWS ( Sales ), "Orders", [OrderCount], "CustomersWithSales", DISTINCTCOUNT ( Sales[CustomerKey] ) )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Eight SaleID values represent eight lines.
2. Order 101 repeats across two lines, leaving seven orders.
3. The fact includes customer keys 10, 20 and 30.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Revenue | Lines | Orders | CustomersWithSales |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 187 | 8 | 7 | 3 |

Counting Customer rows would produce four, which is a valid catalog population but a different metric.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Paper's overlapping order population

**Starting context:** Paper products 1 and 2; all years and customers otherwise allowed.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "Revenue", [NetRevenue], "Lines", COUNTROWS ( Sales ), "Orders", [OrderCount], "CustomersWithSales", DISTINCTCOUNT ( Sales[CustomerKey] ) ), Product[Category] = "Paper" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Five eligible lines total 91.
2. Their order keys are 101, 101, 201, 202 and 204.
3. All three purchasing customers still appear, but only four distinct orders remain.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Revenue | Lines | Orders | CustomersWithSales |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 91 | 5 | 4 | 3 |

A lower revenue total does not imply fewer distinct customers. One population can remain unchanged while another metric changes substantially.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

1. Customer 20 and 30 can appear in both groups.
2. Remove both lines from order 101.
3. Choose the table before choosing the count function.
4. Look for customers crossing category boundaries.
5. BLANK is included in its distinct domain.
6. A user can be active on several days.
