# Fixed effects and latent random effects

Start with: tmb-010, bayes-010.

Outcome: Distinguish a latent joint objective from its marginal likelihood.

The classroom companion runs in the prepared R statistics runtime. It uses base R to expose the mathematics or data contract. Compiling and executing TMB uses the separate [owned project](../projects/tmb-local/README.md); a companion run is not a TMB compiler or rpy2 receipt.

Repeated measurements within a group often share an unobserved influence. A random-intercept model writes
\[
y_{ig}=\beta+u_g+\epsilon_{ig},\quad u_g\sim N(0,\tau^2),\quad \epsilon_{ig}\sim N(0,\sigma^2).
\]
The joint density includes both observation terms and the normalized density of every latent intercept. The fixed parameters are \(\theta=(\beta,\log\sigma,\log\tau)\). The latent vector \(u\) is integrated out when constructing a marginal likelihood for \(\theta\).

`random="u"` in `MakeADFun` selects this integration behavior. Omitting it makes `u` part of the optimizer's joint parameter vector. These objectives are different, even when they use the same C++ template. Penalized fitting of latent modes is not generally maximum marginal likelihood.

### Worked case A: exact Gaussian marginal covariance

Within one group of size \(m\), the marginal covariance is \(V=\sigma^2I_m+\tau^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\). Two distinct observations have covariance \(\tau^2\), while each has variance \(\sigma^2+\tau^2\). The companion constructs this matrix and evaluates a normalized multivariate Gaussian NLL by a Cholesky factorization.

This provides a valuable oracle: the latent Gaussian integral is exactly quadratic, so the Laplace approximation is exact up to numerical tolerance. Our compiled acceptance compares TMB's integrated value and gradient to this independent dense calculation. Agreement here is stronger than comparing two implementations of the same approximate optimizer call.

### Worked case B: unequal information and shrinkage

For known \(\beta,\sigma,\tau\), the conditional mode and mean are
\[
\hat u_g=\frac{m_g\tau^2}{\sigma^2+m_g\tau^2}(\bar y_g-\beta).
\]
A group with two measurements is shrunk more than a group with twenty measurements under the same variances. The companion prints both shrinkage factors. The amount of shrinkage is not an arbitrary smoothing preference; it follows from the specified joint model and known hyperparameters. Estimated hyperparameters add uncertainty.

The mean-zero latent distribution anchors the fixed intercept. Including an unrestricted fixed dummy for every group and an unrestricted random intercept for the same group creates redundant explanations unless constraints or a different model resolve them. A model that fits perfectly can still be unidentified.

### Diagnostics and answered discussion

**Are random effects always Bayesian?** A probabilistic latent distribution can be used within marginal likelihood estimation of fixed parameters. A full Bayesian analysis also assigns priors to those parameters and integrates their uncertainty. **Can I split observations from one group across independent fits?** Not if they share the same latent effect. Keep the complete group together or compute the correct joint contribution. **Do random effects have to be Gaussian?** TMB can approximate suitable continuous latent integrals, but Gaussian approximation quality and differentiability matter. Discrete states require explicit marginalization or a different algorithm.

Chapter 13 derives the curvature correction that separates joint-mode fitting from integration.

### Complete R companion

```r
y <- c(.2, .8, 1.1); beta <- .5; sigma <- .4; tau <- .7
V <- sigma^2 * diag(length(y)) + tau^2 * matrix(1, length(y), length(y))
R <- chol(V); residual <- y - beta
nll <- .5 * (length(y) * log(2 * pi) + 2 * sum(log(diag(R))) + sum(backsolve(R, forwardsolve(t(R), residual)) * residual))
print(list(covariance = V, marginal_nll = nll))
m <- c(2, 20); shrink <- m * tau^2 / (sigma^2 + m * tau^2)
print(data.frame(group_size = m, shrinkage_factor = shrink, latent_mode = shrink * .8))
stopifnot(shrink[2] > shrink[1], all(eigen(V, symmetric = TRUE)$values > 0))
```
