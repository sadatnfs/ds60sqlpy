## The question and the mechanism

Build an experiment-quality semantic report without treating repeated events or displayed estimates as causal proof.

An experiment report must preserve the randomized or assigned unit. Assignments has one row per unit, Outcomes one finalized outcome per assigned unit, and Exposures one row per event. Repeated exposure events are not extra independent assignments. Missing exposure records also do not automatically remove an assigned unit from an intention-to-treat denominator.

The synthetic Control arm has two assigned units and one conversion; Treatment has two assigned units and two conversions. Treatment's two exposure events both belong to U3, while U4 has no recorded exposure event. A naive event-level summary can hide this coverage defect because its event count still equals two. Report both distinct exposed units and event rows.

The supplied statistical summary contains marginal 95-percent Wilson score intervals for each arm's binomial rate. They are wide because n=2. These intervals are not an interval for the treatment difference and do not validate random assignment, measurement quality, independence or a causal claim. DAX can aggregate and display the model's data, but it does not establish those study-design assumptions.

## Worked cases

### Case A: Assignment and exposure integrity

**Starting context:** Analytics experiment fixture; all four assigned units have one complete outcome; exposure is a separate repeated-event stream.

```dax
EVALUATE SUMMARIZECOLUMNS ( Assignments[Arm], "Assigned", COUNTROWS ( Assignments ), "Converted", SUM ( Outcomes[Converted] ), "ExposedUnits", DISTINCTCOUNT ( Exposures[UnitID] ), "ExposureEvents", COUNTROWS ( Exposures ), "AssignedUnitRate", DIVIDE ( SUM ( Outcomes[Converted] ), COUNTROWS ( Assignments ) ) )
ORDER BY Assignments[Arm]
```

**Context trace:**

1. Control U1/U2 yield one conversion among two assignments.
2. Treatment U3/U4 yield two conversions, but both treatment exposure events belong to U3.
3. Assigned-unit rates are 0.5 and 1; exposure counts reveal a separate coverage/duplication issue.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Arm | Assigned | Converted | ExposedUnits | ExposureEvents | AssignedUnitRate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 0.5 |
| Treatment | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |

The observed difference 0.5 is a descriptive contrast in this synthetic fixture, not evidence that the assignment or measurement process supports causal inference.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Display supplied uncertainty without overstating it

**Starting context:** Supplied marginal 95 percent Wilson score intervals for each arm's binomial rate; n=2 per arm; not a difference interval.

```dax
EVALUATE SELECTCOLUMNS ( ExperimentSummary, "Arm", ExperimentSummary[Arm], "Rate", ExperimentSummary[Rate], "WilsonLower", ExperimentSummary[WilsonLower], "WilsonUpper", ExperimentSummary[WilsonUpper] )
ORDER BY [Arm]
```

**Context trace:**

1. Control has k=1,n=2 and estimate 0.5.
2. Treatment has k=2,n=2 and estimate 1.
3. The marginal intervals remain wide; an all-successes sample does not imply a known population rate of 1.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Arm | Rate | WilsonLower | WilsonUpper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control | 0.5 | 0.09453120573 | 0.9054687943 |
| Treatment | 1 | 0.3423802275 | 1 |

The interval table has its own method and assumptions. Displaying it accurately is different from validating the scientific design or calculating a treatment-effect confidence interval.

Hand-derived/displayed supplied statistical summary. No DAX or statistical runtime was executed by this lesson.

## A deeper model or scope review

The supplied intervals use the Wilson score construction with
\(z=1.959963984540054\) and \(\widehat p=k/n\):

\[
c=\frac{\widehat p+z^2/(2n)}{1+z^2/n},\qquad
h=\frac{z\sqrt{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)/n+z^2/(4n^2)}}{1+z^2/n}.
\]

The marginal bounds are \(c-h\) and \(c+h\), limited to the probability range
for numerical reporting. The fixture supplies the two arm-specific summaries.
These are not confidence limits for the treatment-control difference, and two
units per arm provide extremely weak evidence. A real experimental analysis
also needs random assignment, exposure and outcome definitions, missingness
checks, a declared estimand and an appropriate uncertainty procedure. DAX can
reconcile the unit-level data without substituting for that statistical design.

## Progressive hints

1. The assigned population and distinct exposed identities do not change.
2. That changes the estimand and conditions on a post-assignment process.
3. Include unit identity, assignment, outcome completeness and event integrity.
4. A sample of two successes still has substantial uncertainty.
5. The target contrast has its own sampling distribution and design.
6. Follow-up opportunity and population definitions remain explicit.
