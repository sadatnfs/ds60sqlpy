## The question and the mechanism

State which filters a share denominator removes and which it retains.

A share is not defined by the word percent. Its denominator might be the category, the selected products, all catalog products, a region or the entire model. REMOVEFILTERS makes one part of that population change explicit. Removing Color leaves Category in place; removing the Product table clears its product attributes while an independent Customer filter can remain.

This difference explains apparently inconsistent percentages. Notebook's 67 is about 73.6 percent of Paper's 91, but it is about 35.8 percent of all revenue 187. Neither percentage is inherently the correct one. The correct denominator follows the business question, which must be written before the formula.

A broad table modifier can have consequences beyond one visible column because model relationships and expanded-table semantics matter. Begin with narrow dimension modifiers when that is the intended scope. Do not remove filters from a large fact table as an unexplained shortcut to 'all data'. The advanced model lessons examine cases where that shortcut changes more than expected.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

The numerator uses the current context. The denominator reevaluates [NetRevenue] after removing only the named filters. DIVIDE preserves undefined ratios as BLANK. Customer filters are not removed by the Product-only modifier in the second case.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **REMOVEFILTERS**, **denominator scope**, **column versus table**, **share**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
```

### Case A: Share within the retained category

**Starting context:** Paper and Blue selected; remove only Color in the denominator.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "Numerator", [NetRevenue], "CategoryDenominator", CALCULATE ( [NetRevenue], REMOVEFILTERS ( Product[Color] ) ), "Share", DIVIDE ( [NetRevenue], CALCULATE ( [NetRevenue], REMOVEFILTERS ( Product[Color] ) ) ) ), Product[Category] = "Paper", Product[Color] = "Blue" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. The numerator contains Notebook: 67.
2. Removing Color restores Pen within Paper, giving 67 + 24 = 91.
3. The share is 67/91; Home remains outside the category population.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Numerator | CategoryDenominator | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 67 | 91 | 0.7362637363 |

The denominator does not become 187 because the Category filter was deliberately retained.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: All products within North

**Starting context:** Paper, Blue and North selected; remove Product filters only in the denominator.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "Numerator", [NetRevenue], "RegionalDenominator", CALCULATE ( [NetRevenue], REMOVEFILTERS ( Product ) ), "Share", DIVIDE ( [NetRevenue], CALCULATE ( [NetRevenue], REMOVEFILTERS ( Product ) ) ) ), Product[Category] = "Paper", Product[Color] = "Blue", Customer[Region] = "North" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. North Notebook sales are S1 and S4: 47.
2. Removing Product restrictions admits North Pen 24? In North Pen contributes S2 4 plus S5 20, totaling 24, and Mug contributes 36.
3. The retained North population totals 47 + 24 + 36 = 107; share is 47/107.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Numerator | RegionalDenominator | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 47 | 107 | 0.4392523364 |

An 'all products' denominator can still be regional and temporal. The model does not interpret the word all as an instruction to remove every other dimension.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

1. No other dimension filters exist in that case.
2. Keep the year in both numerator and denominator.
3. Name both removed and retained filters.
4. Recall where the scalar was bound.
5. A blank numerator and a valid broader population are different facts.
6. Retain the relevant company, currency and period.
