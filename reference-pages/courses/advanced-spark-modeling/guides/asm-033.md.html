asm-033: Independent latent groups and Laplace sums#

Tutorial mode. Stage: advanced. Direct prerequisites: asm-032, tmb-013.

Starting point and result#

TMB can apply a Laplace approximation to selected random effects. If latent variables are independent by group conditional on shared parameters, their marginal likelihood can factorize by group. This provides a real route for Spark-assisted TMB evaluation, but it requires a proof about the model's latent structure, not merely a convenient group key.

Worked case: independent random intercepts#

Suppose each group has its own normal random intercept and no cross-group covariance. The group-laplace project case evaluates complete groups at common fixed hyperparameters, compares group-summed output with a serial oracle and retains inner optimization diagnostics. For a Gaussian random-intercept model, the marginal distribution is available analytically, making it an especially strong small reference.

The Laplace approximation is exact for the Gaussian integral in this case. That exactness does not extend to every count or nonlinear latent model. The example validates the decomposition and implementation while later models require approximation-quality checks.

Worked case: shared hyperparameters still need a global optimizer#

Even when the integrals factor, the variance hyperparameter may be shared across all groups. Optimizing it independently in every group and averaging estimates does not maximize the sum of marginal log likelihoods. A valid outer optimizer broadcasts one theta, gathers all group values/gradients, combines them and proposes the next theta.

Warm-starting inner modes can reduce cost, but cached state must be bound to the group and compatible model/data version. Retries and worker replacement must reconstruct valid state. A warm start is an optimization aid, not a different objective or permission to stop the inner solve prematurely without diagnostics.

Failure diagnosis#

An indefinite inner Hessian invalidates the ordinary positive-definite Laplace formula at that candidate. A failed inner optimizer must not be returned as a successful group contribution. Missing a latent prior term changes the model and often destroys the intended curvature. A group count mismatch can make the outer objective appear artificially favorable.

Interview discussion#

Does a common theta prevent group parallelism? No. Hold theta fixed during an evaluation, compute independent group marginal contributions, then combine them before updating theta.

Can you sum group Hessians to obtain the full marginal Hessian blindly? Only when the terms and derivative definitions correspond to the same shared parameterization and complete marginal objective. Inner latent Hessians are not the same object as the outer parameter Hessian.

Worked reference: Group-separable Laplace calculation#

This is an editable reading/project reference. It has no ordinary pad Run action.

When conditional groups factor, the joint marginal density is the product of group integrals. The group Laplace negative log likelihood adds joint density at the inner mode, half the inner Hessian log determinant, and minus q/2 times log(2*pi).

For independent Gaussian random intercepts, the integral is Gaussian and Laplace is exact. This gives a useful analytic oracle. Shared hyperparameters still require one common candidate vector and global outer optimization. Independent group hyperparameter optima are not generally averaged into the global optimum.
