## Averaged gradients require explicit mass accounting

A distributed optimizer should optimize the same declared objective as its single-process reference. PyTorch DistributedDataParallel ordinarily averages gradients across ranks. If every rank contributes the gradient of its own local mean, that average equals the global observation mean only when local observation masses are equal, or when the objective intentionally weights ranks equally.

Let rank \(p\) have \(n_p\) observations and local mean loss \(L_p\). With \(W\) ranks and total count \(N=\sum_pn_p\), the desired global mean is

\[
L=\sum_p\frac{n_p}{N}L_p.
\]

If the reducer averages rank gradients, give each rank the local scalar

\[
\widetilde L_p=\frac{Wn_p}{N}L_p,
\quad\text{so}\quad
\frac1W\sum_p\nabla\widetilde L_p=\nabla L.
\]

For fixed observation weights, replace counts by weight mass. All ranks must agree on the same global denominator and parameter state. This identity describes the ordinary fixed-world-size averaging contract; specialized communication hooks and uneven-input join behavior need their documented semantics checked separately.

## Worked case 1: unequal local sample sizes

The complete program uses \(y=(0,2,4,8,10,12)\), a common mean parameter \(\mu=3\), and row loss \((y_i-\mu)^2/2\). Split the first two observations onto one conceptual rank and the remaining four onto another. The global mean-loss gradient is -3. The local mean gradients are 2 and -5.5, whose unweighted average is -1.75.

Scale the local gradients by \(Wn_p/N\) before averaging, and the result becomes -3, matching the full-data oracle. The example obtains gradients from actual Torch tensors but performs the rank reduction serially in one CPU process. It validates the scaling algebra. It does not initialize a process group, execute collectives, qualify multi-host training or measure distributed speed.

A penalty adds another scaling decision. A global regularizer added to every rank's local objective appears once after a gradient average, but appears \(W\) times after a gradient sum. If the entire local objective is scaled for unequal data mass, a penalty inside it may receive unintended weights. Derive the complete data-plus-penalty objective before choosing placement.

## Worked case 2: inspect sampler membership

Use five data indices with two ranks and Torch's actual `DistributedSampler`, with shuffling disabled so membership is easy to inspect. With `drop_last=False`, the sampler pads to equal rank lengths, causing index zero to appear twice. With `drop_last=True`, the tail index four is omitted instead. Neither policy means that exactly five distinct observations contribute once to an epoch.

Padding may be reasonable for stochastic neural training, but an exact full-data likelihood audit must account for repeated observations. A repeated record can be assigned an appropriate correction weight or excluded through a coordinated design, provided every rank still participates correctly in required collectives. Dropping records changes the fitted dataset and must be disclosed. Changing the seed or calling `set_epoch` affects shuffle order, not the basic equal-length padding issue.

## Minibatches and objective meaning

A uniformly sampled minibatch mean provides an unbiased estimate of the full observation-mean gradient under suitable sampling assumptions. It is not a deterministic full-likelihood evaluation suitable for an ordinary L-BFGS line search. Gradient accumulation over several batches must use their actual masses; averaging batch means equally misweights a smaller final batch. For dependent panels, sample complete independent panels or derive a valid conditional contribution rather than cutting arbitrarily through a joint covariance likelihood.

Empty ranks and unequal step counts are execution hazards as well as arithmetic hazards. A rank that skips a collective while others call it can hang a job. Correct serial scaling cannot prove the distributed process lifecycle is correct. That requires actual multi-process tests, timeout behavior, failure recovery and source-bound runtime evidence in the integration workshop.

## Answered review questions

**Can Lightning remove the need to understand loss scaling?** No. A training framework manages execution and conveniences, but the author still owns the objective, reduction, sampling units and penalty semantics. Inspect the framework's active distributed strategy and sampling behavior.

**Does a larger global batch require multiplying the likelihood?** It depends on whether the declared objective is a sum or mean and how gradients are reduced. State that contract before applying learning-rate rules.

**What is established by this chapter's output?** Correct unequal-mass gradient arithmetic and observed membership from the installed sampler implementation. Actual distributed training is a separate acceptance tier, and this single-process program deliberately makes that boundary explicit.
