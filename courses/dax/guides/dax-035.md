## The question and the mechanism

Define a fiscal year explicitly and distinguish fiscal-to-date from a completed fiscal year.

A fiscal-year label is a convention over dates, not an intrinsic property of an integer called Year. Our ordinary FiscalYear ends in June: July 1,2024 begins FY 2025, while June 30,2024 ends FY 2024. Labeling a fiscal year by its ending year is a deliberate rule that should be written in the date dictionary.

A fiscal-year-to-date question also needs an as-of date. FY 2025 spans July 2024 through June 2025, but this fixture's sales observation ends February 28,2025. Summing available FY 2025 facts gives 123; calling that a completed-year total would overstate the temporal coverage. A complete calendar table can represent future fiscal dates without supplying their outcomes.

Classic time-intelligence functions can support shifted Gregorian year boundaries with their documented year-end arguments. The reference here instead shows the explicit fiscal column and as-of date filter so the included population is easy to inspect. More irregular calendars need additional metadata and, potentially, the version-sensitive calendar-based features introduced next.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

Case A inspects two neighboring boundary dates and their stored FiscalYear labels. Case B applies an explicit fiscal-year filter and a maximum available date. The code does not infer a financial reporting standard from the word fiscal; the fixture's convention is the authority.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **fiscal year**, **date role**, **year label**, **as-of boundary**, **calendar policy**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
```

### Case A: Two neighboring fiscal labels

**Starting context:** The declared fiscal year starts July 1 and is named by its ending year.

```dax
EVALUATE SELECTCOLUMNS ( FILTER ( 'Date', 'Date'[Date] IN { DATE ( 2024, 6, 30 ), DATE ( 2024, 7, 1 ) } ), "Day", 'Date'[Date], "FiscalYear", 'Date'[FiscalYear] )
ORDER BY [Day]
```

**Context trace:**

1. June 30 belongs to the fiscal year ending in 2024.
2. The next day starts the fiscal year ending in 2025.
3. A one-day boundary changes the fiscal label without changing the Gregorian year.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Day | FiscalYear |
| --- | --- |
| 2024-06-30 | 2024 |
| 2024-07-01 | 2025 |

Using Gregorian Year for both rows would miss the fiscal transition. A label convention belongs in the model specification.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Available fiscal-year-to-date revenue

**Starting context:** FY 2025 under the July-start convention, observed only through 2025-02-28; active OrderDate.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "FiscalYTDRevenue", [NetRevenue] ), 'Date'[FiscalYear] = 2025, 'Date'[Date] <= DATE ( 2025, 2, 28 ) )
```

**Context trace:**

1. The fiscal interval starts July 1,2024.
2. The as-of boundary stops at February 28,2025, not June 30.
3. The available matching sales are S4..S8, totaling 123.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| FiscalYTDRevenue |
| --- |
| 123 |

There happen to be no second-half 2024 sales in this fixture. That does not make Gregorian and fiscal-year measures generally equivalent.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

1. It lies between July 2024 and June 2025.
2. The included dates can stay the same while their label changes.
3. Include the fiscal convention and observation date.
4. Align the follow-up boundary or disclose the difference.
5. Week/period membership requires an explicit calendar mapping.
6. A posted period and an event date can have distinct meanings.
