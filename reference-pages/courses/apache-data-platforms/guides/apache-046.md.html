apache-046: Event time and watermarks#

Tutorial mode. Stage: intermediate. Direct prerequisites: apache-045, apache-007.

Event time is carried by the data#

Processing time is the worker's clock. Event time is the timestamp assigned to an observation by its source contract. They answer different questions. A purchase can be processed late because of a network delay without changing when the purchase occurred. Windows must state which clock defines their boundaries, which timestamp unit is used and how events outside a completeness policy are handled.

A watermark is an estimate that event-time progress has passed a position. It is not proof that no older event can arrive. A strategy such as maximum observed timestamp minus a delay chooses a practical completeness/latency tradeoff. With several input partitions, an idle partition can hold back combined progress unless idleness is handled explicitly. Advancing a watermark too aggressively can make legitimate delayed records late.

The workshop uses an actual bounded Flink source with explicitly controlled record timestamps and watermark commands. It does not wait for wall-clock chance to create a late event. Milliseconds are the declared unit, and two-second tumbling windows use half-open intervals [0,2000)[0,2000)[0,2000) and [2000,4000)[2000,4000)[2000,4000). This makes every result and rejection independently traceable.

Worked case A: out-of-order records before progress advances#

The timeline emits east records at 1000 and 1500 milliseconds, then a west record at 500. Although the third record is older than the previous two, no watermark has yet closed its window. All three belong to the first two-second window. The source then advances its watermark to 2000. The actual window operator emits east total 11 from two records and west total 9 from one record.

The checker compares window start, end, key, count and amount, not only the grand total. An implementation using processing time might accidentally produce a plausible overall sum while assigning it to the wrong window. Keeping exact boundaries in the oracle makes that mistake visible even on a tiny fixture.

Flink's window trigger uses its defined maximum timestamp convention at the window end boundary. The source's chosen watermark passes that threshold deliberately. This is a deterministic mechanism experiment, not a benchmark or a statement that two seconds is an appropriate production delay policy for every dataset.

The fixed trace makes the accepted/late boundary explicit:

Source action Event time in ms Amount Observation
east order 1 1000 4 first window, accepted
east order 3 1500 7 first window, accepted
west order 2 500 9 out of order, still accepted
watermark 2000 none first window closes
east order 4 1000 5 arrives after closure, late output
east order 5 2500 3 second window, accepted
watermark 4000 none second window closes

Worked case B: an event arrives after its window closed#

After the watermark reaches 2000, the source emits another east record timestamped 1000 with amount 5. Its event time still belongs to the first window, but the configured zero allowed-lateness policy has already closed that window. The job routes the event to the explicitly named late side output. It must not silently add the amount to an unrelated current window or discard it without an observation.

A later east record at 2500 with amount 3 belongs to the second window. Advancing the watermark to 4000 closes that window with total 3. The checker expects three normal window results and one identified late record. These are separate outputs with different meanings, rather than a single sum that could conceal misplaced data.

Allowed lateness can permit updates to a window for some time after its first result. Downstream systems must then understand correction or changelog semantics. A side output can support a reconciliation workflow, but routing alone does not reprocess the record or establish a business close policy. State the intended correction path before selecting a lateness duration.

Diagnosis and interview discussion#

Record source timestamps, watermark progress, window boundaries, lateness policy and output identities. Inspect partition-level progress and idleness when a window appears stuck. A quiet input may be delaying progress without any record being invalid. A final maximum watermark from a bounded source is different from an unbounded production source promising completeness forever.

Interview discussion: can an out-of-order event still be on time? Yes. It can arrive before the relevant watermark/lateness boundary closes its window. The two cases use real Flink window processing to distinguish an older record accepted before progress from a later arrival routed after closure, with exact interval and identity checks.
