## A nonlinear mean is a scientific statement

An exponential decay with a nonzero baseline is a useful model for processes that approach a stable level:

\[
m(t;a,b,c)=a e^{-bt}+c,\qquad a>0,\ b>0.
\]

Here \(a\) is the initial excess above baseline, \(b\) controls the rate of decay and \(c\) is the long-run baseline. Time units matter: changing days to hours changes the numerical value of \(b\). The half-life is \(\log(2)/b\). A decay curve is not automatically an appropriate model for seasonal, growing or intervention-driven data; its interpretation must match the mechanism and observation window.

Use optimizer coordinates \(\theta=(\log a,\log b,c)\) and a known observation standard deviation \(\sigma\). Conditional independent Gaussian observations give

\[
\mathcal L(\theta)=n\log\sigma+\frac n2\log(2\pi)
 +\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i[y_i-m(t_i;\theta)]^2.
\]

The transform enforces positive amplitude and rate without a parameter-Jacobian term. The baseline is unconstrained here. If the application requires a positive baseline, a different parameterization would encode that requirement and change its possible boundary behavior.

## Worked case 1: recover a known nonlinear curve

The program constructs 25 observations at equally spaced times from zero to six using exactly \((a,b,c)=(2.4,0.65,0.7)\). It supplies \(\sigma=0.2\) and optimizes all three mean parameters from a different initial curve. Because the fixture is noiseless and the scale is fixed, it has a transparent optimum with zero residuals and avoids the unbounded free-scale problem described in Chapter 5.

The returned physical parameters should agree closely with the generating values, and the maximum absolute score should be small. This is a numerical recovery oracle. It is not a simulation study of statistical consistency, confidence-interval coverage or real forecast accuracy. The fixed scale is part of the optimization fixture; the data do not estimate it. A report that labels this result as successful uncertainty calibration would be scientifically incorrect.

The code then evaluates the fitted mean at times seven and nine, beyond the observed window. These are extrapolations under the same decay law. The predicted values approach 0.7 because the model encodes that asymptote. A visually smooth curve beyond the observed range provides no independent evidence that the mechanism remains unchanged there.

## Worked case 2: shorten the observation window

Keep the same parameters but observe only times from zero to 0.08. The first-order expansion is

\[
a e^{-bt}+c\approx(a+c)-ab t.
\]

Over a sufficiently short window, the data mainly reveal the combinations \(a+c\) and \(ab\), not all three parameters separately. Many amplitudes, rates and baselines produce almost the same values. This is weak identification, even though the function and its derivatives are perfectly valid.

The program forms the Jacobian of the mean with respect to the three optimizer coordinates and compares its largest and smallest singular values. For the long window, the ratio is about 6.1; for the short window it is about 27,212. These values describe sensitivity geometry for this fixture, not universal thresholds. The short window makes a direction in parameter space much less visible to the observations. The profile-likelihood chapter will show the same issue from a different perspective.

## Practical fitting and failure diagnosis

Inspect time units, parameter scale and the range of observed times before tuning optimizer settings. Starting with an enormous decay rate makes most exponentials effectively zero, leaving almost no rate sensitivity. Starting with an extremely small amplitude similarly makes the decay shape difficult to learn. Multiple plausible starting values help distinguish poor initialization from a stable optimum, but repeated convergence to similar values cannot create missing scientific information.

Use residual patterns to challenge the independent Gaussian assumption. Consecutive positive residuals followed by consecutive negative residuals can indicate temporal dependence or an inadequate mean. Fit/forecast splits must respect time. Randomly shuffling future records into training can yield optimistic performance even when the numerical optimizer is correct. For multiple subjects, decide whether parameters are shared, separately fitted or hierarchical before averaging curves.

## Answered review questions

**Why can predictions inside the observed window be stable while parameters are unstable?** Distinct parameter vectors can produce nearly identical mean curves over a narrow interval. Prediction at observed times may be identified better than individual mechanism parameters or extrapolation.

**Does nonlinear least squares differ from Gaussian MLE?** With independent constant known variance, the objectives differ only by a positive scale and constant, so the mean-parameter minimizers coincide. Unknown scale, dependence or non-Gaussian observations require the appropriate likelihood terms.

**What belongs in a forecast report?** Time units, training window, parameterization, residual assumptions, convergence diagnostics, uncertainty method and a clear distinction between interpolation and extrapolation. This small CPU program validates arithmetic and recovery, not a deployed forecasting service.
