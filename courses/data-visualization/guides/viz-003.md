# viz-003: Draw uncertainty without changing its meaning

## Level and prerequisites

Beginner. Direct prerequisites: `viz-002`, `statistics-008`. Use the prepared Python figure capture or the JavaScript browser variant.

## What an error bar represents

An error bar needs a named quantity, construction and coverage interpretation. Standard deviation describes observations; standard error describes an estimator; confidence intervals concern a parameter; prediction intervals concern future outcomes. They can look similar while answering different questions.

The fixture contains three prespecified regional mean differences -.4,.2,.8 points with SEs .1,.3,.2. Construct separate normal-approximation 95% intervals using estimate plus/minus 1.96 SE. Their bounds are [-.596,-.204], [-.388,.788] and [.408,1.192]. These are marginal intervals, not a 95% simultaneous guarantee across regions.

## Worked case: an effect plot

Use a horizontal point trace with regions on y, effects on x, and horizontal error bars. Add a vertical zero reference. North is negative under this approximation, Central remains uncertain around zero, and South is positive. Preserve region labels rather than encoding region solely through color. Hover text and the source table give both interval endpoints.

Plotly's error array contains distances from the center, not absolute endpoint coordinates. For an asymmetric interval, use `array = upper - estimate`, `arrayminus = estimate - lower`, and `symmetric=False`. Passing the upper endpoint itself would draw the wrong uncertainty while producing a valid figure.

## Contrasting case: a practical threshold

Suppose improvement must exceed .5 points. South's lower bound .408 does not establish that stronger claim, despite excluding zero. Add a clearly labeled practical reference if it supports the decision. Do not replace uncertainty with green/red labels that conceal margins or interval width.

A treatment comparison across subgroups also needs multiplicity and interaction reasoning. One significant subgroup and one nonsignificant subgroup do not prove their effects differ. A direct contrast with appropriate covariance addresses that question; the chart cannot infer it from two p-value labels.

## Six progressive exercises

1. **Calculate bounds.** Derive all three intervals by hand. Expected evidence: endpoint table matching the fixture. Hint: multiply SE by 1.96 before adding or subtracting.
2. **Build error distances.** Supply the correct Plotly arrays. Expected evidence: rendered endpoints equal the calculated bounds. Hint: distances and coordinates are different.
3. **Add a practical margin.** Mark .5 and describe South's evidence. Expected evidence: positive versus zero but inconclusive above .5. Hint: compare the lower endpoint, not just the center.
4. **Debug an asymmetric interval.** For estimate .8 and bounds [.3,1.5], repair arrays incorrectly set to [.3,1.5]. Expected evidence: minus distance .5 and plus distance .7. Hint: subtract around the center.
5. **Change the uncertainty target.** Given outcome SD two and n=100, contrast SD two with mean SE .2. Expected evidence: a clearly relabeled figure, never an unlabeled substitution. Hint: a narrow mean interval does not describe most individuals.
6. **Transfer to a family.** Explain how three separately valid intervals differ from a simultaneous family. Expected evidence: an explicit family definition and a possible Bonferroni construction. Hint: adjust the critical level, not the effect estimate.
