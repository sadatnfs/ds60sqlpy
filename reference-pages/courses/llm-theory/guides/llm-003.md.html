Purpose and mental model#

A sequence probability is built from conditional probabilities. The first token has a probability under its start context, the next has a probability given the preceding prefix, and later tokens depend on longer prefixes or a model's restricted context. Multiplying those conditional values follows the probability chain rule; multiplying unrelated marginal token frequencies answers a different question.

For a fixed three-target demonstration, the supplied conditional probabilities are 0.5, 0.25 and 0.8. Their product is 0.1. A negative log likelihood adds the negative logarithms instead, yielding log(10). It is a score of the supplied observed sequence under that conditional table, not a probability that a story is true.

Working in log space matters for long sequences because a direct product can round down to zero even when every factor is positive. A zero assigned probability is a different case: the observed event is impossible under the supplied distribution, so its negative log score is infinite. We will keep those two observations distinct and label whether a reported loss is a total or a mean over target positions.

Vocabulary and concepts#

Prefix: the preceding tokens supplied as context before scoring the next target.

Conditional probability: the probability of an event given its stated context, not a claim that successive tokens are independent.

Likelihood: the probability the model assigns to the observed target sequence under its conditions.

Negative log likelihood: the sum of negative natural logarithms of target probabilities; smaller values assign more probability to the observation.

Underflow: a floating-point product becoming zero because its positive mathematical value is too small for the representation.

Syntax or reasoning anatomy#

For probabilities p1, p2 and p3, the product is p1p2p3. The corresponding total negative log likelihood is -log(p1)-log(p2)-log(p3). math.log uses the natural logarithm. A mean divides that total by the number of scored target positions, which must be recorded. The logarithm identity improves arithmetic stability; it does not repair probabilities computed from the wrong contexts.

A helper should accept a nonempty list of finite values between zero and one. Reject an invalid negative value, a value above one, and NaN. Treat an explicit zero as infinite negative log likelihood instead of quietly clipping it to an arbitrary positive value. Preserve a finite log-space total even when the separately reported direct product underflows. Do not infer factual correctness or independent evidence from a favorable likelihood.

Worked example / walkthrough#

Example A: Trace a three-target score#

Use the supplied conditional table as fixed data, not three independent token marginals.

import math

probabilities = [0.5, 0.25, 0.8]
likelihood = math.prod(probabilities)
nll = -sum(math.log(p) for p in probabilities)
assert math.isclose(likelihood, 0.1, rel_tol=0, abs_tol=1e-12)
assert math.isclose(nll, math.log(10), rel_tol=0, abs_tol=1e-12)
print(likelihood, nll)

Expected: The total is log(10), while the per-target mean is log(10)/3. A different prefix could change any later conditional; the list is not a universal token-frequency table.

Example B: A finite log score after product underflow#

Repeat a small positive conditional probability many times. Do not replace a zero printed product with a claim that the supplied probabilities were zero.

probabilities = [0.01] * 1000
likelihood = math.prod(probabilities)
nll = -sum(math.log(p) for p in probabilities)
assert likelihood == 0.0
assert math.isfinite(nll)
assert math.isclose(nll, 1000 * math.log(100), rel_tol=0, abs_tol=1e-9)
print(likelihood, nll)

Expected: The direct product underflows to zero in float64/Python float, but the log-space total remains finite. This is a numeric representation limit, not an impossible event in the declared table.

Exercises#

  1. Trace the fixed conditionals. Derive the product, total negative log likelihood and scored-target count for [0.5,0.25,0.8]. Expected: Likelihood 0.1, total NLL log(10), and three targets. Verify: Assert the product and log score within 1e-12 and name each preceding context in a short table.
  1. Change the conditioning assumption. Compare the supplied table with [0.5,0.5,0.5], which incorrectly reuses one unconditional frequency for all positions. Expected: The alternative product is 0.125 and its total NLL is 3*log(2), different from log(10). Verify: Record both products and explain that the changed conditionals define a different model, not an algebraic shortcut.
  1. Preserve a long log score. Implement sequence_score(probabilities) and test 1000 copies of 0.01 without summing logs of an already multiplied product. Expected: Direct product is 0.0 from underflow; total NLL is finite and equals 1000*log(100) within 1e-9. Verify: Assert the finite log-space value and keep the underflowed product labeled separately.
  1. Handle certain and impossible targets. Score [1,1] and [0.5,0]. Do not replace zero with an undisclosed epsilon. Expected: The all-one score has NLL 0; a zero-probability target gives product 0 and NLL positive infinity. Verify: Use math.isinf for the impossible-event result and compare it with the finite underflow result from Exercise 3.
  1. Reject invalid probabilities. Test an empty list, [-0.1], [1.1] and [NaN]. Expected: All four cases raise ValueError with a nonempty/range/finite explanation. Verify: Capture each exception separately, then assert that the original three-target score still matches log(10).
  1. Declare an aggregation unit. Document A has target probabilities [0.5]; document B has [0.25,0.25,0.25]. Compare token-weighted mean NLL and equal-document mean NLL. Expected: Token mean is 7log(2)/4; equal-document mean is 3log(2)/2. Verify: Write the four-target denominator and the two-document denominator explicitly and assert both values within 1e-12.

Progressive hints#

  • Hint 1: Write the prefix beside every target probability before multiplying.
  • Hint 2: Take logs of each positive factor before adding; do not log a product that may already be zero.
  • Hint 3: Label whether your denominator counts targets or documents and handle an explicit zero as its own case.

Common pitfalls#

If a product prints zero, inspect the individual probabilities. A thousand positive factors can underflow; a supplied factor of zero represents an impossible observed event under the declared model. Those cases share a printed product but differ in their log score. If an implementation computes log(product) after multiplication, it has already lost the stability benefit.

If two loss reports disagree, inspect whether one is a sequence total, token mean or equal-document mean. A changed tokenizer changes which targets are counted, so a token-based score cannot be casually compared across different representations. High sequence likelihood is also not an answer-verification method: it measures the model's assigned probability to text, not the truth of its content.
