## Starting point and result

A nonlinear mean function can still yield an additive likelihood. We model a decaying signal with baseline: \(y_i=a\exp(-bt_i)+c+\epsilon_i\), with independent Gaussian errors of variance \(\sigma^2\). This example fits a finite grid of decay rates and profiles out the linear coefficients and variance. It is a genuine grid-constrained likelihood fit, not a claim of continuous global optimization.

## Mechanism: profile parameters that have closed forms

For a fixed candidate \(b\), set \(u_i=\exp(-bt_i)\). The mean is linear in \(a,c\). The normal equations are

\[\begin{bmatrix}\sum u_i^2&\sum u_i\\\sum u_i&n\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a\\c\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum u_i y_i\\\sum y_i\end{bmatrix}.\]

Spark computes six statistics for each candidate: count, sum of u, sum of u squared, sum of y, sum of uy and sum of y squared. The driver solves the two-by-two system and computes residual sum of squares. At a positive residual sum of squares, the Gaussian variance MLE is SSE/n, not SSE/(n-2). The latter is an unbiased variance estimator under a different purpose.

Substituting the variance estimate gives \(L_{\mathrm{profile}}(b)=n[\log(2\pi\,\mathrm{SSE}(b)/n)+1]/2\). This objective includes the variance normalization. Minimizing only squared standardized residuals while freely increasing sigma would produce a broken objective.

## Worked case: recovering a decay curve

Eight observations have true amplitude two, decay rate 0.4 and baseline 0.5, with small deterministic residuals. Spark crosses those rows with five rates from 0.2 through 0.6. This bounded cross join creates forty rows. Grouping by the candidate rate produces five sufficient-statistic summaries. Each is compared with a separately computed serial profile fit.

The selected grid rate is 0.4. The output includes amplitude, baseline, variance and profile loss for every candidate, so the reader can inspect how sharply the data distinguish nearby rates. This does not establish a confidence interval or prove that a continuous optimizer would choose exactly 0.4. A finer grid or continuous search is a separate computation.

## Worked case: nonidentifiability at one time

If every time is zero, every u is one. The amplitude and baseline columns coincide: only a+c is identifiable. The determinant \(n\sum u_i^2-(\sum u_i)^2\) is zero. The helper rejects this system before dividing. More executors cannot identify parameters that the design does not distinguish.

Near-zero determinants also arise when times cover an extremely narrow interval. Use centering, scaling, better experimental design or a model with fewer free parameters. In larger systems prefer numerically stable QR or SVD methods over manually formed normal equations. This two-variable example exposes the algebra rather than recommending normal equations universally.

## Failure diagnosis

A negative SSE from the expanded summary formula may reflect cancellation when residuals are tiny compared with signal size. Do not hide it with unconditional clipping. Recompute residuals directly for a diagnostic candidate or use more stable centered summaries. Exact fits with SSE zero do not have a finite positive Gaussian scale MLE under this model.

## Interview discussion

**Does nonlinear mean imply a non-additive likelihood?** No. Independence determines factorization. Nonlinearity changes optimization and conditioning, not whether row contributions can be summed.

**Why not cross join millions of rates?** The grid expands work multiplicatively. A driver optimizer can request a few adaptive candidates instead; it then pays repeated action latency. Choose using measured scan and scheduling costs.
