# tf-001: A bounded deep-learning investigation

## Level and prerequisites

Beginner within this subject; estimated time: 75 minutes.

## Learning objectives

Distinguish fitting a function from evidence that it generalizes. By the end, you can produce the exact observations below,
explain one plausible wrong result and transfer the reasoning to a new fixed
fixture without copying a solution. Keep your first prediction alongside the
actual evidence so a changed conclusion remains visible.

## Purpose and mental model

A training example is information used to choose a rule. A held-out example is reserved for inspecting the chosen rule afterward. The distinction is a decision policy, not a special property of an array or a filename. Once you use an evaluation result to choose another weight, threshold or feature, that result has participated in selection. This lesson makes that boundary visible before a framework can hide it behind a score.

Our published demonstration has training IDs t1, t2, t3, features 1, 2, 3 and targets 2, 4, 6. Its demonstration holdout has IDs h1, h2, h3, h4, features 4, 5, 6, 7 and targets 8, 10, 12, 14. Each row is one observation. For now every row has its own source-group ID. These numbers are public teaching material, so they are not a blind examination. A teacher needs a different undisclosed fixture to assess independent transfer.

Compare a rule chosen from training only with a rule that was repeatedly changed after inspecting holdout mistakes. Both may print the same final error, but they support different claims. A low error also says nothing about inputs outside this small range, private-data handling, fairness or future population changes. Start by naming the exact observation unit and the use you intend to investigate.

## Vocabulary and concepts

**Observation:** one paired feature/target record with an identity; three numbers in a row are not automatically three independent observations.

**Target:** the supplied value the prediction is compared with; it stays fixed during evaluation.

**Training split:** the records allowed to influence fitted parameters or preprocessing statistics.

**Holdout:** records reserved under a declared policy for a later evaluation; tuning after inspection changes their role.

**Baseline:** a deliberately simple reference rule, such as the training-target mean, that gives a model score context.

## Syntax or reasoning anatomy

Read a report in this order: identity of the evaluated rows, the already chosen prediction rule, residual for each paired row, reduction and denominator, then the limited claim. A squared residual has squared target units. An average over four records divides by four; it is not interchangeable with an average over three training records. A source group can contain related rows. Disjoint row IDs do not establish independent groups.

For written work, keep a small table with columns row ID, source group, split, feature, target and prediction. Add the decision that selected the rule before the score. Do not erase an earlier failed prediction after you see the answer. The purpose is to make reasoning inspectable, including a justified decision that the available evidence is insufficient.

## What can a perfect fit leave unknown?

The rule y_hat = 2x matches every published row in this demonstration. Another
rule, y_hat = 2x + (x-1)(x-2)(x-3), also matches every training row because
one factor vanishes at each of x = 1, 2 and 3. At x = 4, however, its added
term is 6, so it predicts 14 instead of 8. Perfect training fit alone cannot
choose between these rules. Model assumptions and independent observations
are doing work that the training score cannot do.

The demonstration holdout distinguishes these particular rules, but repeatedly
inventing new rules after reading all its outcomes uses it for selection.
A new untouched assessment would be needed for a fresh final claim. This is
why the split policy belongs to the sequence of decisions, not merely to
separate file names. In practice, begin by stating whether the next prediction
concerns a new record from a known customer, a previously unseen customer,
or a future time. Each setting changes which overlap is acceptable.

## Worked example / walkthrough

### Example A: A training-only constant baseline

The training-target mean is \(\frac{2+4+6}{3}\) = 4. Keep that constant fixed when you inspect the demonstration holdout.

```text
Training residuals:  4-2=2, 4-4=0, 4-6=-2
Training MSE: (4+0+4)/3 = 8/3
Holdout residuals: 4-8=-4, 4-10=-6, 4-12=-8, 4-14=-10
Holdout MSE: (16+36+64+100)/4 = 54
```

**Expected:** The two means measure the same fixed rule on different sets. The holdout target mean would be 11, but using it to choose the constant would break this training-only comparison.

### Example B: Different row IDs can still leak a source

Suppose training row t1 and holdout row h1 were two measurements from account A. Their names differ, but their shared account can reveal information about the evaluation case.

```text
train:   row=t1, group=A
holdout: row=h1, group=A
Row-ID intersection: empty
Source-group intersection: A
Decision: reject this grouped split for an account-held-out claim.
```

**Expected:** The diagnosis starts with group IDs, not a more complicated model. If the intended claim concerns new accounts, split by account before fitting statistics.

## Exercises

1. **Name the units and roles.** Create the seven-row demonstration table above. Mark which rows may influence a fitted mean and which are evaluated later.
   **Expected:** Three training rows and four holdout rows; the row-ID and group-ID intersections are empty.
   **Verify:** Inspect the row-count table and assert the two ID-set intersections are empty.

2. **Compare a fixed baseline.** Calculate the training-target mean without using holdout labels, then compute its training and holdout MSE.
   **Expected:** The constant is 4, training MSE is \(\frac{8}{3}\) and holdout MSE is 54.
   **Verify:** Record all seven residuals and compare their two separately labeled sums and denominators.

3. **Separate a sum from a mean.** For predictions [7,11] and targets [8,10], calculate the two residuals, squared-error sum and MSE. State the target units.
   **Expected:** Residuals [-1,1], squared-error sum 2 and MSE 1; squared target units.
   **Verify:** Assert the sum equals twice the mean for these two records; do not compare 2 with another dataset mean.

4. **Find a grouped leak.** Change only h1's group to the same group as t1, retaining different row IDs. Explain the evaluation claim that now fails.
   **Expected:** Row IDs remain disjoint but one source group overlaps; a new-source-group claim is rejected.
   **Verify:** Inspect both intersections separately and retain the named overlapping group in the report.

5. **Refuse an empty score.** Remove every holdout row. Decide whether its MSE is zero, perfect, or undefined, and design the smallest useful rejection message.
   **Expected:** An empty evaluation set has no mean; the reference raises ValueError instead of reporting zero.
   **Verify:** Capture the empty-input exception and confirm the original four-row evaluation still yields 54 afterward.

6. **Bound the conclusion.** A rule \(y=2\cdot x\) has zero error on all seven published points. Write a defensible conclusion and one unsupported conclusion, then name a fresh assessment.
   **Expected:** The supported statement concerns those seven published points only; all-future accuracy or production-safety claims remain unsupported.
   **Verify:** The evidence note names seven observations, the already visible rule, and a new source/time fixture that has not influenced selection.

## Progressive hints

- **Hint 1:** Write the ID and source group beside every row before calculating a metric.
- **Hint 2:** Choose the constant from the training targets, then hold it fixed while subtracting each evaluation target.
- **Hint 3:** Inspect the denominator and the row/group intersections separately; a filename cannot establish a split policy.

## Common pitfalls

If an error looks unexpectedly small, first list every decision that used evaluation labels. A preprocessing mean fit on all seven targets is already a changed experiment. If group leakage appears, changing row names does not repair it; rebuild the split by the source unit and start selection again. If a report says zero error with no rows, inspect its denominator rather than accepting the flattering score.

A deterministic example is useful for learning the bookkeeping. Determinism does not create a representative sample, and seven perfectly aligned values do not establish behavior on a different population. Keep the published fixture labeled demonstration evidence. Accuracy, privacy and safety are different questions with different evidence needs.

## Self-check

Close the table and explain why a holdout is a decision policy. Then change the training targets to [1,3] and the evaluation target to [5]. Derive the fixed training-mean prediction and its single squared error without looking at the reference.

## Evidence review

Use a 0, 1 or 2 score for each of five dimensions: the declared data/observation
contract, a correct normal-case derivation, diagnosis of the changed fixture,
explicit handling of the invalid case, and a fresh explanation of the evidence
boundary. Normally seek \(\frac{8}{10}\) with no zero for the contract or claim boundary,
then require a new transfer example. This is a human learning rubric, not an
automatic certification or completion awarded by a single successful Run.
