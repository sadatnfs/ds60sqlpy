nlp-021: Text leakage, duplicates and evaluation design#

Level and prerequisites#

Advanced; about 120 minutes plus discussion. Direct prerequisites: nlp-020. Read the preceding guide if its notation or information boundaries are unfamiliar.

Purpose and mental model#

Text evaluation is especially vulnerable to hidden dependence. One source document can generate many paragraphs, paraphrases, translations, summaries or ticket updates. Randomly placing these rows in different splits can make a model appear to generalize while it recognizes a template or sees nearly the same evidence twice. The statistical unit must match the deployment question.

Exact duplicate detection, near-duplicate detection, family grouping and time-aware splitting solve related but different problems. A hash can identify the same normalized bytes, a similarity score can propose related text, and provenance can establish that several records came from one underlying source. None should be silently substituted for the others.

The reference builds a tiny provenance-aware split and a family-level paired bootstrap. It exposes a semantic near miss with high token overlap and shows how row weighting differs from equal-family weighting. These mechanisms support an honest evaluation design; a five-document fixture cannot establish a stable deployment performance interval.

Define the generalization target first#

A future-message evaluation asks how a model performs after a time cutoff, potentially for customers or templates already seen. A new-family evaluation asks how it performs on unseen source families. A future-new-family evaluation requires both boundaries. They can produce different test sets and answer different questions.

The fixture has family a at times one and two, family b at times one and three, and new family c at time three. With cutoff two, the training set contains a1,a2,b1. Future b2 is a valid known-family future case but is excluded from the future-new-family test, which contains only c1. Retain the excluded ledger so the filtering does not look like silent deletion.

A strict split can leave too little evaluation data. That is a limitation to report, not a reason to relax the boundary without changing the claim. A larger prospective collection may be needed. Choosing the split after observing which yields the best score turns evaluation design into model selection.

Duplicates and semantic near misses#

The normalized hash here casefolds and collapses whitespace before hashing. Reset the router and reset the router therefore match. This is a declared equivalence policy, not a universal content identity. Raw bytes and original text should remain available because case or spacing can matter in some domains.

Jaccard similarity compares token sets through intersection size divided by union size. Service is ready and service is not ready score three-quarters, yet disagree on the key fact. Near-duplicate thresholds should generate review candidates or provenance groups, not automatically merge contradictory records. Bag-of-token similarity also ignores frequency and order.

For large collections, approximate methods such as MinHash or locality-sensitive hashing can help find candidate overlaps, but the final policy still needs task-aware validation. Deduplication can remove evaluation leakage while also changing population weights, so record what was merged, excluded and retained.

Fit the entire pipeline inside the boundary#

Vocabulary, IDF, tokenizer training, feature selection, calibration and hyperparameter tuning all learn from data. Fitting them on the full collection before splitting can leak information even if the final classifier is trained only on training rows. Use a pipeline or an equivalent explicit procedure, and keep source-family metadata separate from predictive features unless the deployment task truly permits it.

Language-model adaptation adds near-duplicate demonstrations, generated paraphrases and retrieval-index curation as additional leakage paths. A test question may not appear verbatim in training while its source document and answer template do. Preserve source IDs and time/version information so a reviewer can reconstruct what the system could know.

A held-out set used repeatedly for prompt revisions becomes a development set. Keep final acceptance separate or use an evaluation design that accounts for adaptive selection. Do not claim a frozen benchmark while changing its difficult labels or dropping failed cases without a documented reason.

Compare systems with the right resampling unit#

For paired systems, compute a per-case loss or success difference on the same cases. When variants share a family, aggregate or resample at the family level rather than treating every variant as independent. A clustered bootstrap samples families with replacement and carries their within-family information together.

The fixture family mean differences are .2,-.1,.3, giving equal-family mean 215\frac{2}{15}. Flattening all six rows instead gives .2 because the positive families contain more variants. These are different estimands. The bootstrap uses a fixed seed for reproducibility, but three families are far too few for a strong population uncertainty claim.

Report the number of independent families, variation across them, weighting convention and uncertainty method. A narrow row-level interval can be a symptom of duplicated evidence rather than high confidence in generalization.

Worked examples#

Worked example 1: Find an exact normalized duplicate#

Casefolding and whitespace collapse make the two router texts identical before hashing. The original strings are preserved. A readiness sentence with not remains a different hash, showing that this policy does not delete that meaningful token.

Worked example 2: Keep time and family exclusions explicit#

At cutoff two, train IDs are a1,a2,b1, the future-new-family test contains c1 and b2 appears in an excluded-known-family ledger. Both time ordering and family disjointness are asserted. The one-case test is transparently too small for a broad performance claim.

Worked example 3: Compare two weighting targets#

Family means [.2,-.1,.3] average to .13333, while row-level differences average to .2 because family c has three rows. The seeded bootstrap resamples the three family means and yields repeatable quantiles. It illustrates dependence-aware arithmetic, not reliable inference from three groups.

Executable worked traces#

Run these two small blocks in order. They reproduce the numerical or structural observations above; the full reference additionally checks the exercise changes and failure cases.

import hashlib
normalize=lambda text:' '.join(text.casefold().split())
a=hashlib.sha256(normalize('Reset the router').encode()).hexdigest()
b=hashlib.sha256(normalize('reset  the router').encode()).hexdigest()
print(a==b)
assert a==b
import numpy as np
groups={'a':[.2,.2],'b':[-.1],'c':[.3,.3,.3]}
family=np.mean([np.mean(values) for values in groups.values()])
rows=np.mean([value for values in groups.values() for value in values])
print(family,rows)
assert np.isclose(family,2/15) and np.isclose(rows,.2)

Interview reasoning#

Interview: Why is random text-row splitting dangerous? Related paragraphs, paraphrases or updates can share the same source evidence across splits. The model may recognize a family rather than generalize to an unseen one.

Interview: Does a hash solve semantic deduplication? It detects equality under a stated normalization. Similar meaning, contradictions and paraphrases need a different policy and often review.

Interview: Why bootstrap families? When rows within a family are dependent, resampling them independently overstates information. The unit should match the claim and population weighting.
