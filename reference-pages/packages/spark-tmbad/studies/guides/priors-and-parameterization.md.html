Priors, penalties and the quantity being estimated#

A prior is a probability distribution on a declared parameter space. A penalty can have a prior interpretation, but only after its coordinates, normalization, weighting and placement are specified. Begin by writing the density or objective; matching a software argument name is not enough.

Four distinct operations#

  1. Marginal maximum likelihood: a generative random effect uuu has a density such as N(0,τ2)N(0,\tau^2)N(0,τ2). Integrating it out produces p(y∣θ)=∫p(y∣u,θ)p(u∣θ) dup(y\mid\theta)=\int p(y\mid u,\theta)p(u\mid\theta)\,dup(y∣θ)=∫p(y∣u,θ)p(u∣θ)du. Maximizing this over population parameters is still marginal ML.
  2. Marginal MAP: add a declared prior p(θ)p(\theta)p(θ) to that marginal likelihood and optimize −log⁡p(y∣θ)−log⁡p(θ)-\log p(y\mid\theta)-\log p(\theta)−logp(y∣θ)−logp(θ). This returns one mode.
  3. Random-effect Laplace: approximate the integral over uuu around its conditional mode. This numerical method does not, by itself, introduce a prior over the population parameters or return their posterior.
  4. Parameter-posterior inference: normalize/sample/approximate p(θ∣y)p(\theta\mid y)p(θ∣y). A Gaussian posterior approximation around a MAP, exact conjugate posterior, and MCMC are distinct methods with distinct checks.

Our current native generic Laplace implementation concerns item 3. Its conditional random-effect moments condition on fitted population parameters. Calling those moments a complete Bayesian posterior prediction would omit another uncertainty layer. The current Torch Gaussian random-intercept model uses an exact Gaussian integral, not a generic latent Laplace engine.

Study interpretation and interview discussion#

Does a random-effect prior make the fitted model fully Bayesian? No. It may be part of a generative mixed model whose latent effects are integrated for ML. Population-parameter priors and their posterior uncertainty are separate choices.

Can I compare a penalized native growth fit with a Torch decay fit after changing coordinates? Unpenalized likelihoods can describe the same curve under a bijection. An isotropic L2 penalty usually changes under that transformation, so reusing the same lambda does not preserve the MAP target.

Why can a 95% Bayesian credible interval miss a fixed true parameter too often? A strong prior can be poorly centered at that particular truth. Prior-predictive calibration and fixed-truth frequentist coverage ask different questions. The simulation must report its parameter-generating regime.

The pinned glmmTMB prior vignette explicitly discusses MAP and a separately requested posterior sampler. Its parameter-scale conventions must be checked before attempting a comparison. The historical AFModel helper named prior_coefs_mean performs post-fit sign filtering. Such selection is neither a density prior nor automatically valid post-selection inference.
