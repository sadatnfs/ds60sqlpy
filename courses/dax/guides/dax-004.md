## The question and the mechanism

Predict asymmetric filter propagation and identify when a SQL join analogy breaks.

A relationship connects typed columns and declares cardinality, active status and filter direction. The engine uses it when determining eligible rows. It does not promise that selecting any fact row will also shrink every connected dimension. In our single-direction star, a Product selection filters Sales, but a direct Sales filter does not flow backward to Product.

That asymmetry is useful: dimensions remain stable descriptive populations while facts record activity. Counting products in the Product table answers how many dimension products remain visible; it does not necessarily count products sold in the currently filtered fact rows. For products actually sold, count Sales[ProductKey] or deliberately construct the matching set.

A materialized SQL join has different mechanics. A duplicate dimension key can multiply joined fact rows, while a regular one-to-many model relationship requires uniqueness on its one side. Missing foreign keys can create an engine blank member rather than the same rows returned by a naive inner join. Use SQL as an independent arithmetic check only after aligning the population and missing-key policy.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

The paired queries count Product and Sales in the same outer context. COUNTROWS(Product) is not a measure of sales activity. Filtering Sales[SaleID] leaves Product's single-direction incoming eligibility unchanged. Bidirectional filtering would change this behavior and can introduce ambiguous paths, so do not enable it merely to make one number look convenient.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **relationship direction**, **one side**, **many side**, **relationship versus join**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
```

### Case A: Dimension-to-fact propagation

**Starting context:** Blue Product rows; active Product -> Sales direction.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "VisibleProducts", COUNTROWS ( Product ), "Lines", COUNTROWS ( Sales ), "Revenue", [NetRevenue] ), Product[Color] = "Blue" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Notebook and Mug are Blue.
2. Their keys admit six sales lines.
3. Product count is 2; their revenues 67 and 96 sum to 163.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| VisibleProducts | Lines | Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 6 | 163 |

The selected dimension supplies a key set that travels to the fact. It is not inferred from which products happen to have sales in another visual.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Fact filter does not flow backward

**Starting context:** Direct filter Sales.SaleID = S4; no Product filter; single-direction relationships.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "VisibleProducts", COUNTROWS ( Product ), "Lines", COUNTROWS ( Sales ), "Revenue", [NetRevenue] ), Sales[SaleID] = "S4" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Sales is reduced to S4.
2. The relationship does not propagate from its many side to Product.
3. All four Product rows remain visible, even though S4 uses only product 1.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| VisibleProducts | Lines | Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 1 | 27 |

A product-count card showing four is consistent with the declared model. The mistake would be naming that count 'products sold in this line'.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

1. Count keys in the filtered fact.
2. Bag has no sales.
3. Place both scalar counts in ROW.
4. A change in model semantics affects more than that cell.
5. The one-side uniqueness requirement concerns identity.
6. An inner join removes the unmatched event.
