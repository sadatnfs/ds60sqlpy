## The question and the mechanism

Trace a dimension selection through an active relationship to the exact contributing fact rows.

A star schema separates descriptive dimensions from event facts. A product category is described once per product; transaction rows keep the corresponding product key. A filter on the dimension first restricts its visible keys, then an active relationship propagates those keys to the fact. It does not physically create a permanent joined copy of both tables.

Our model uses regular one-to-many relationships with filters flowing from Product and Customer into Sales. Paper admits ProductKey 1 and 2. North admits CustomerKey 10 and 30. Applying both selections means a sales line must satisfy both sets. The filters intersect at the fact; two slicers do not mean 'Paper OR North' unless an expression explicitly defines that different question.

Model design is already part of calculation design. If a category is copied inconsistently into different sales rows, a filter on that fact attribute can answer a different question than the dimension's current category. Historical classification may be a legitimate requirement, but it needs a declared versioned dimension or event-time attribute. Do not hide that decision in an unexplained formula.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

CALCULATETABLE supplies the stated filters around a one-row result table. Product[Category] and Customer[Region] are column references with table qualification. Two filter arguments are applied together. The relationship metadata, not a textual resemblance between column names, determines which Sales rows they reach.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **star schema**, **dimension**, **foreign key**, **filter propagation**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
```

### Case A: Paper products

**Starting context:** Product.Category = Paper; other dimensions unrestricted.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "Revenue", [NetRevenue] ), Product[Category] = "Paper" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Paper maps to product keys 1 and 2.
2. Eligible lines are S1, S2, S4, S5 and S7.
3. 20 + 4 + 27 + 20 + 20 = 91.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Revenue |
| --- |
| 91 |

The result includes several customers and years. A category selection does not implicitly select the latest year or a region.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Paper in the North

**Starting context:** Product.Category = Paper AND Customer.Region = North.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "Revenue", [NetRevenue] ), Product[Category] = "Paper", Customer[Region] = "North" )
```

**Context trace:**

1. North admits customer keys 10 and 30.
2. Intersect those customers with Paper's product keys.
3. S7 is excluded because customer 20 is South; 91 - 20 = 71.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Revenue |
| --- |
| 71 |

A relationship graph plus eligible-row list explains the reduction without guessing which visual control caused it. Both filters remain part of the measure's question.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

2. Only the product filter changes.
3. Use arrows for filter direction, not SQL join syntax.
4. Those populations overlap.
5. Customer 40 has no Sales rows.
6. Current customer attributes are not automatically historical attributes.
