# tf-024: Capstone: a small classifier with honest evaluation

## Learning objectives

Deliver a bounded classifier with a baseline, leakage checks and error analysis. The central investigation is concrete: Generate local geometric arrays with frozen seeds and group IDs; train at most two small hidden layers and compare a simple baseline on an untouched test split. The finish condition is Report confusion counts summing to the test cardinality, per-group errors, training-only preprocessing and the chosen validation decision. No promised accuracy threshold substitutes for these checks. A useful explanation connects the calculation to the reason it is needed, rather than treating a function name as a definition.

## Purpose and mental model

A classifier experiment needs more than an accuracy number. Define which rows are independent, freeze training/validation/test groups, fit preprocessing only on training data and choose decisions before opening the test partition. The fixture uses the sign of a geometric coordinate so its labels can be inspected directly. A tiny linear sigmoid classifier is sufficient; extra hidden layers are optional, not a requirement. Confusion counts retain the error types and must sum to the test cardinality. Per-group reporting reveals failures that an aggregate can hide. A capstone is complete when its data and evaluation contracts are defensible, even if its measured score is disappointing.

## Vocabulary and concepts

**Confusion counts:** Counts of correct and incorrect positive and negative decisions.

**Decision policy:** A frozen rule converting scores into predictions.

**Test unit:** The held-out entity counted once in final evaluation.

## Syntax anatomy and reasoning sequence

Binary logits feed sigmoid cross entropy during training. After a fixed step budget, classify with logit>=0, equivalent to probability>=.5. Compute true-positive, false-positive, true-negative and false-negative counts with boolean masks. Keep counts as integers and divide only after establishing their denominators. Save the split IDs before any training operation.

## Read the classifier as an experiment

The training inputs [-2, -1, 1, 2] have mean zero and population standard
deviation sqrt(2.5). Those training statistics define the transformation used
for every later record. Keeping a held-out record's original ID alongside its
transformed value lets you check both the numerical operation and the
information boundary. The two checks answer different questions.

The supplied training loop has a fixed budget of 40 updates. Its purpose is
to demonstrate a complete small experiment with a baseline, a fitted model
and an assessment on two reserved rows. One true positive and one true
negative give accuracy 2/2 on those rows. A constant predictor makes one
mistake. This establishes the observed comparison, with a denominator of two;
it does not establish a narrow population error bound or calibrated scores.

Classification accuracy and cross entropy can tell different stories even
on the same records. Once two logits fall on the correct sides of zero,
their classifications may stop changing while their probabilities and losses
continue to change. On perfectly separable data, unregularized logistic
training can keep increasing coefficient magnitudes to reduce cross entropy
without improving already perfect training accuracy. A fixed update budget
is therefore part of the experiment, not just a convenience for the worker.

Inspect false positives and false negatives separately when the downstream
costs differ. Keep any subgroup denominator beside its metric, including
groups with no observations. Use validation data for threshold or architecture
selection; preserve a separate final assessment if those choices are tuned.
This tiny capstone makes the bookkeeping visible before a larger dataset
makes accidental leakage harder to see.

## Worked example / walkthrough

### Example A: Freeze geometry and groups

The toy target is fully specified, so a learner can reason about errors without an opaque dataset. The holdout still has only two observations and cannot support broad conclusions.

```python
import numpy as np
import tensorflow as tf

x=np.array([[-2.],[-1.],[1.],[2.]],dtype='float32'); y=np.array([[0.],[0.],[1.],[1.]],dtype='float32')
test_x=np.array([[-3.],[3.]],dtype='float32'); test_y=np.array([0,1]); train_ids={'a','b','c','d'}; test_ids={'e','f'}
assert not train_ids&test_ids and len(test_ids)==2
```

**Expected:** 4 train and2 test records with no shared IDs.

### Example B: Fit training preprocessing

The test coordinates never enter the mean or scale calculation. A symmetric result is expected here because the training fixture itself is symmetric.

```python
import numpy as np
import tensorflow as tf

x=np.array([[-2.],[-1.],[1.],[2.]],dtype='float32'); y=np.array([[0.],[0.],[1.],[1.]],dtype='float32')
test_x=np.array([[-3.],[3.]],dtype='float32'); test_y=np.array([0,1]); train_ids={'a','b','c','d'}; test_ids={'e','f'}
assert not train_ids&test_ids and len(test_ids)==2
mean=x.mean(0); scale=x.std(0); tx=(x-mean)/scale; vx=(test_x-mean)/scale
np.testing.assert_allclose(scale,[np.sqrt(2.5)],atol=1e-6,rtol=0)
assert mean[0]==0 and vx[0,0]==-vx[1,0]
```

**Expected:** mean0; scale sqrt2.5; test transformed symmetrically.

## Common pitfalls and diagnosis

If training improves but held-out decisions do not, inspect the
train-only transform and the ID join before changing model capacity. If
accuracy stays fixed while loss moves, inspect logits rather than assuming
the optimizer stopped. Report the two-row assessment as two observed cases;
do not turn the exact fixture result into a population guarantee.
