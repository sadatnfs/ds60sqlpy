# llm-030: Pretraining data provenance and evaluation contamination

## Level and prerequisites

Advanced; approximately 120 minutes. Direct prerequisites: `llm-029`. The prerequisite sequence introduces the notation used here; revisit its worked examples if an axis, probability or gradient is unfamiliar.

## Learning objectives

Specify provenance and removal policies before training or evaluating on collected text. The central investigation is concrete: Build a manifest for local authored snippets with source, permission, split and duplicate-family fields; analyze a benchmark-answer contamination case. The finish condition is All fixture documents have provenance and a split. Benchmark answers and derived paraphrases are excluded from the training/selection source set. A useful explanation connects the calculation to the reason it is needed, rather than treating a function name as a definition.

## Purpose and mental model

<!-- textbook-advanced-start -->

### Make the path from source material to training examples inspectable

A dataset is not merely a directory of text files. Each example has a source, a version, a transformation history and a role in an experiment. Provenance records those relationships so a reader can ask why the example was included and which conclusions depend on it. This chapter uses authored local snippets, allowing the mechanics of the record to be studied without collecting external data or making permission decisions about an unknown corpus.

A useful document identity contains more than its filename. A file can be renamed without changing its bytes, and its bytes can change while the name stays the same. A cryptographic content hash identifies the exact bytes that were hashed, subject to the normal collision-resistance assumption. A source identifier says where those bytes were obtained or authored. A version or acquisition record says which instance was used. None of these fields independently establishes a permission, privacy or evaluation-independence claim.

#### Trace one document through a pipeline

Imagine source document d1, an authored policy paragraph. The raw UTF-8 bytes are hashed as h_raw. A normalization step changes line endings and applies a declared Unicode policy, producing h_normalized. A chunker divides that text into spans c1 and c2, each with source offsets and its own content hash. A tokenizer then turns the spans into integer sequences under tokenizer version v1. These are related artifacts with different identities.

If a model later fails on a question about c2, the lineage record should allow the reader to recover the exact source version, normalized text, selected span and tokenization. Replacing every identity with one generic "dataset hash" would make that diagnosis harder. Conversely, recording every temporary byte without explaining the transformations would produce a large but unhelpful log. The goal is a traceable chain of meaningful artifacts and decisions.

The worksheet's strings `hash-of-d1` and `hash-of-d2` are placeholders indicating where actual digests belong. They are not cryptographic receipts. A real record must compute the digest from the bytes actually admitted. Hashing a filename or a display preview while training on different content would bind the wrong artifact.

#### Distinguish admission, transformation and experiment role

Admission asks whether a source belongs in the intended corpus under the applicable project policy and task constraints. Transformation asks how accepted content is cleaned, normalized, segmented or labeled. Experiment role asks whether an artifact is used for training, development, calibration, retrieval context or final assessment. These decisions interact, but a positive answer to one does not settle the others.

For example, an authored public snippet can be harmless to store and still be unsuitable for a final holdout if its paraphrase was used to choose prompts. A source may be technically readable but lack an adequate record supporting the intended use. A duplicate may be permitted to retain for audit while excluded from a particular training calculation. Treat the fields as separate evidence, rather than compressing them into one unexplained `approved=True` flag.

This chapter is about data and experiment bookkeeping. It does not infer legal permission from a source label or provide a legal determination. The fixed local fixture lets us inspect the manifest contract itself: source, declared permission basis, split, family and actual content identity should remain visible.

#### Exact deduplication is only one relation

An exact hash match detects identical byte sequences. Normalized hashes can detect equality under a chosen canonicalization policy. Near-duplicate methods can identify related wording, and family labels can connect paraphrases, templates or documents derived from one source. Each relation has a different meaning and error profile. A missing exact hash match does not establish semantic independence.

Consider three documents: d1 is an original explanation, d2 is a reformatted copy, and d3 is a paraphrase of d2. Exact-byte deduplication may connect none of them. A normalization rule may connect d1 and d2. A reviewed derivation relationship may connect d2 and d3. For a family-disjoint split, the transitive relationship matters: d1 and d3 should not be placed in opposite partitions merely because they were not compared directly.

A family graph makes this explicit. Nodes are artifacts and edges record a relevant relationship such as exact copy, normalized copy, paraphrase or shared template. Connected components can be used conservatively as split units when the relationship policy justifies it. Incorrect edges can overmerge unrelated examples, while missed edges can leave leakage. Retain the relationship type and review basis so a split can be audited instead of treating a cluster label as unquestionable truth.

#### Evaluation contamination can enter through selection

Training on a final assessment answer is an obvious information leak, but it is not the only path. Repeatedly examining test failures to choose prompts, retrieval rules, thresholds or adaptation hyperparameters uses that set for development. The model weights may remain untouched while the overall system has still been selected using evaluation information.

The worksheet's candidate d3 is derived from the answer to evaluation document d2. Renaming it or assigning a new row ID does not remove that relationship. Under the stated experiment, it is blocked from both training and selection sources pending review. The important fact is the information flow, not the filename. A clean evaluation boundary requires tracking which artifacts influenced which decisions.

Open-book retrieval evaluation needs an additional distinction. A source document containing the factual answer may be deliberately available to a RAG system; that can be the task's intended evidence. The benchmark's authored reference answer, scoring rationale or hidden labels are different artifacts and should not silently become retrieval context. Define whether the experiment tests retrieval from permitted source documents, memorized knowledge, or both. "The answer appears somewhere in the corpus" is not enough to classify every case without the task contract.

#### Version changes can invalidate a previous result

Suppose a retrieval corpus is updated after a policy revision. A cached answer generated from the old version may be factually obsolete even though its text and citation ID remain unchanged. The system needs to bind its answer evidence to a source version or content identity, not merely to a stable human-readable title. Training-data provenance and live retrieval provenance address different time horizons, but both rely on identifying which content was used.

A tokenizer or chunker update can also change downstream examples without modifying the raw documents. New chunk boundaries may move the supporting sentence outside the selected context window. Different normalization can merge or separate duplicates. When a metric changes after such an update, the experiment should be able to distinguish data selection, representation and model changes. Preserve the previous record rather than overwriting it with the current pipeline configuration.

Deletion and correction requests illustrate why provenance is operationally useful. A source-to-derived-artifact map can identify affected chunks, indexes or training manifests. It does not by itself prove that a trained model has forgotten a source or that every downstream copy was removed. The evidence needed for those claims depends on the actual system and action performed. State exactly which artifacts were located, changed and rechecked.

#### Build a reviewable corpus packet

For the small fixture, the packet can remain compact: two document rows with actual source identities, declared permission basis, family labels, split roles and hashes; one explicit relationship showing that d3 derives from evaluation material; and a decision explaining why d3 is not admitted into training or selection. The decision should name the violated experimental boundary and the observation needed to revisit it.

A trustworthy packet keeps unknowns visible. An unknown source relationship is not the same as a verified independent family. A missing permission record is not an affirmative grant. A successful hash computation is not a privacy review. These distinctions prevent an apparently complete table from implying that every field was independently established.

## Vocabulary and concepts

**Provenance:** A record of origin and transformations for a data item.

**Permission field:** An explicit documented basis or unresolved status for use.

**Derived contamination:** Assessment information entering through paraphrases or transformed copies.

## How to run this lesson

Text blocks here are evidence tables or written arguments, not programs. Keep unknown evidence distinct from a failed condition and explain what observation would settle it.

## Syntax anatomy and reasoning sequence

A useful record has document_id,source,permission,split,family and content_hash. Source identifies origin; permission records an explicit basis or unresolved status. Split states intended information use. Family groups related derivatives. Audit the complete dependency path, including tokenizer fitting and prompt selection, because contamination can occur before gradient training.

## A corpus is a lineage graph, not just a folder

Imagine a source document d2 and an answer key d3 derived from its benchmark question. Even if their byte hashes differ, training on d3 can expose the information that evaluation on d2 was supposed to withhold. The relevant relationship is derivation or shared family, not only exact file equality. A source ID, a content hash, a family ID and a split label record different parts of this relationship. Keep the raw source identity and the transformation history when normalized or chunked derivatives are created.

The short hash labels in this worksheet are illustrative identifiers. They are not actual digest receipts for external datasets. A real manifest would name the digest algorithm and bind the exact raw bytes or the precise normalized representation. Normalization itself needs a versioned rule: case folding, whitespace changes or boilerplate removal may expose duplicates but can also discard information needed by the task. Preserve enough lineage to explain why two items were considered related.

## Information can leak before the optimizer runs

Training examples are one route for contamination. Prompt selection, retrieval-index construction, threshold tuning and repeated manual inspection of held-out answers can also adapt a system to its supposed test set. Keeping benchmark-derived d3 out of gradient updates is insufficient if its answers choose the final prompt or retrieval configuration. Assign families before those choices and keep an evaluation partition reserved for the final declared comparison.

Permission and scientific independence are separate checks. A permission record describes the documented basis and conditions for using material; a split record describes its role in an experiment. Neither is implied by the other. Unknown permission or unknown ancestry should remain explicit rather than being converted into an assumed approval or an assumed clean split. This lesson audits recorded provenance; it does not determine legal rights from a file name.

For a subject assistant, retrieval documents additionally need revision, validity period and access metadata. A document may be permitted for one audience but excluded for another, or remain a valid historical source while becoming unsuitable for a current-policy answer. An exact hash can detect changed bytes, but it cannot decide which policy revision governs a question. The applied course develops those operational checks in [corpus provenance and chunking](../../llm-applied/README.md). The useful output here is a traceable inclusion decision with a reason, not a larger corpus at any cost.

Changing the filename or splitting at the row level does not remove the information transfer. The answer has already exposed the assessment target.

```text

Relationship: evaluation-derived
Training admission: blocked
Selection-source admission: blocked
```

**Expected:** exclude from training and selection sources pending contamination review.

## Common pitfalls and diagnosis

Trace raw and derived records through source, family and split boundaries. A different content hash does not eliminate benchmark ancestry, and a permission label does not prove evaluation independence. Keep unknown lineage explicit. Exclude held-out answers from prompt, retrieval and threshold selection as well as from optimizer updates.
