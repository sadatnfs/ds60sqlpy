Native 0.2 studies: selected priors, exact L1 and honest simulation summaries#

This guide starts from linear regression, Gaussian likelihoods, gradients and basic Spark DataFrames. It shows how to define a selected penalty, fix a known nuisance parameter, check an analytic answer and interpret a retained study receipt. Use the separately prepared native package project. These sim-* names are study-design IDs, not newly registered classroom lessons or ordinary pad jobs.

The initial local Spark smoke passed sim-003 and sim-004 at replication zero, seed 381729. Each contains weak- and strong-slope data, two methods and two reductions, giving 16 accepted fits in total. This is one independent generated dataset per scenario, not sixteen Monte Carlo replications. The retained Gaussian-prior receipt, L1 receipt and source inventory bind the exact executed inputs. The five additional local shards also completed: 22 method records, 21 accepted and one beta-binomial optimizer failure retained. The additional receipts bind those observations. Across those initial seven shards there are 38 method records, not 38 independent Monte Carlo datasets.

A later, separately retained expansion now covers sim-003/004 replications 0 through 15. Its observed aggregate has 16 target/scenario/method/reduction groups, each with 16 independent datasets, and all 256 fit records accepted. The earlier replication-zero examples below remain initial-stage history. A separate eight-dataset expansion now covers the five family/panel/independent-fit studies: 176 method records, 172 accepted and four optimizer failures retained. Its independently derived summary is separate from the named-target aggregate and remains bound to the older JAR used by those actual runs. The initial one-replication examples are preserved below.

Neither observed L1 slope is zero: both sample MLEs exceed the threshold. Exact zeros require a sample slope at or below that threshold, not merely a small generating slope. A separate pure analytic case checks that zero branch; it is not reported as another executed Spark dataset here.

At a positive fitted slope the smooth score is -4, canceled by the L1 subgradient +4. At a zero slope the smooth score must lie within [−4,4][-4,4][−4,4]. Use target.stationarity, not the raw gradient norm, to judge the fit. The observed summed KKT residuals were about 1.33×10−141.33\times10^{-14}1.33×10−14 and 7.11×10−157.11\times10^{-15}7.11×10−15. Replacing L1 by a smoothed absolute value or reusing a smooth gradient stopping rule would change this contract. The improved held-out MSEs in these two realized cases are observations, not a general superiority claim or a reason to select the penalty using this holdout.

Reductions, methods and the two studies deliberately reuse data; they do not multiply the independent replication count. One observed success also cannot establish a zero failure probability; the failure summary retains its finite-sample interval.

For RRR independent generated datasets and dataset summaries qrq_rqr​, the Monte Carlo standard error of their mean is

The aggregator refuses repeated training or future bytes under new replicate or seed labels within a study/scenario. Generating new posterior draws for one fixed dataset does not create repeated-data estimator evidence. It also refuses changed targets, artifacts, source or incomplete method coverage. Large runs require a predeclared replication count and an explicit resource plan; they are not started by these documentation commands.

Conditional estimates use successful replications. Procedural coverage includes all attempted replications, assigning zero coverage when no interval is returned. If every fit fails, conditional coverage has count zero and a null mean; procedural coverage is still zero over the attempted denominator. Gaussian pointwise bands and the event that every band along a requested path covers are separate metrics. The latter is not a calibrated simultaneous 95% band.

For independent-group fitting, distinguish a dataset-level procedure failure from an individual group-fit failure. Two datasets with one failed group among four each have two failed complete procedures but only two failed fits out of eight. replication_failure_fraction, group_fit_counts and group_failure_fraction_per_dataset preserve that distinction. Group-failure MCSE is calculated across independent datasets, not by assuming every group or overlapping horizon supplies an independent Monte Carlo replication.

Structural zeros and overdispersion need their own densities#

For ordinary Poisson truth, the mean held-out NLL was 1.507226371 for Poisson, 1.507229699 for ZIP and 1.594566174 for the hurdle model. Under structural-zero truth, it was 1.196595033, 1.126995988 and 1.190710909, respectively. Each score uses its fitted normalized probability mass, including the hurdle's positive-count normalizer. The ZIP zero predictor and its exposure/count predictor play different roles. A hurdle with a zero predictor using z alone need not reproduce the ZIP zero probability, which also depends on x and exposure.

On beta-binomial truth, binomial and beta-binomial mean held-out NLLs were 3.547296613 and 2.403307362, while their MSEs were 9.789324578 and 9.881705902. The distributional score and mean-prediction error answer different questions. A model can describe dispersion better without winning the realized mean MSE. Neither calculation uses a Gaussian surrogate density.

Diagnose the common mistakes#

  • A lower named-Gaussian training objective may reflect its normalizing constant. Compare common held-out scores and inspect the data/prior components.
  • A large raw score on a fixed nuisance coordinate is not evidence that the free coefficients failed to converge. Inspect the constrained target residual.
  • A nonzero smooth score can be exactly correct at an L1 optimum. Check its subgradient cancellation and summed-target KKT units.
  • Reusing the same rows with a new seed label does not add estimator replications. Preserve the dataset hashes and report the actual simulation unit.
  • A pointwise variance is not a joint predictive covariance or a posterior distribution over the population parameters.
