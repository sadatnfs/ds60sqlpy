llm-040: A statistical evaluation and reliability argument#

Level and prerequisites#

Advanced; approximately 135 minutes. Direct prerequisites: llm-039. The prerequisite sequence introduces the notation used here; revisit its worked examples if an axis, probability or gradient is unfamiliar.

Learning objectives#

Choose paired units and uncertainty before comparing complete systems. The central investigation is concrete: Compare two fixed system result tables by question family using paired resampling; keep prompt and retrieval tuning on validation only. The finish condition is Pairs retain the same question IDs, repeated paraphrases remain in their family, and a final holdout is used once. The report includes failures and an abstention policy. A useful explanation connects the calculation to the reason it is needed, rather than treating a function name as a definition.

Purpose and mental model#

Build a comparison around the unit that can generalize#

A reliability argument is more than a favorable metric. It connects a defined use, a comparison design, observed outcomes, uncertainty and a decision rule. The same arithmetic can support different conclusions depending on which observations are treated as independent and which population the result is meant to describe. This chapter uses a deliberately tiny paired fixture so those choices cannot hide inside a large table.

Let ℓA(i)\ell_A(i)ℓA​(i) and ℓB(i)\ell_B(i)ℓB​(i) be two systems' losses on the same question iii, and define di=ℓA(i)−ℓB(i)d_i=\ell_A(i)-\ell_B(i)di​=ℓA​(i)−ℓB​(i). Negative did_idi​ favors A under this loss convention. Pairing by question identity keeps the input difficulty aligned. If A and B are evaluated on different question sets, their average difference can mix model effects with case selection. Stable IDs are therefore part of the experimental design, not merely a database convenience.

Work through the family-weighting difference#

The fixture has three question differences[-1,-1,1] with families[A,A,B]. The row mean is(-1-1+1)/3=-1/3, favoring A. Family A's mean is-1 and family B's mean is 1. Giving each family equal weight produces(-1+1)/2=0. The disagreement is caused by weighting: family A contributes two rows to the row mean but only one family mean to the second summary.

Neither policy is automatically correct for every application. If the deployment workload truly contains twice as many independent requests of A's type and the sample represents those frequencies, row weighting may answer a useful workload question. If the goal is equal importance across source families, the macro family mean is more appropriate. Choose the estimand, meaning the quantity the study aims to estimate, before selecting the summary that looks best.

Related questions also affect uncertainty. Two paraphrases from one source may share much of their difficulty and failure mechanism. Treating them as independent observations can make a confidence interval appear narrower than the evidence warrants. A family-level design preserves that dependence structure more honestly when families are the independent sampling units relevant to the claim.

Enumerate the tiny bootstrap instead of trusting a smooth plot#

The two family values are[-1,1]. A bootstrap sample of two families with replacement has four equally likely ordered index pairs: AA,AB,BA,BB. Their means are-1,0,0,1. Thus the bootstrap distribution has probability 14\frac{1}{4}41​ at-1,12\frac{1}{2}21​ at 0 and 14\frac{1}{4}41​ at 1. The reference's random resampling approximates this simple discrete distribution; it cannot create additional support values or new independent families.

Increasing bootstrap repetitions from 1000 to 100000 reduces simulation noise in estimating this bootstrap distribution. It does not increase the original sample size from two families. A very smooth histogram or many printed decimal places can disguise that limitation. With so few independent units, percentile endpoints are coarse and should not be presented as a precise population reliability guarantee.

A cluster bootstrap must also match the intended weighting. Resampling entire families and then pooling all their rows weights large sampled families differently from first computing family means and averaging them equally. Both procedures preserve within-family grouping in one sense, but they target different summaries. State whether the analysis estimates a row-weighted workload measure or an equal-family measure, then implement the corresponding resampling rule.

Pairing reduces one source of variation, not every source#

Comparing A and B on the same question can reduce noise caused by question difficulty. It does not remove randomness from model training, generation, retrieval changes or scoring. If the model is stochastic, decide whether the quantity of interest averages over random generations, uses a fixed deterministic policy, or includes training-seed variability. A bootstrap over questions conditional on one trained model does not estimate variation across all possible training runs.

Scoring can also be uncertain. Human judgments may disagree, and an automatic judge may have systematic preferences. Preserve the rubric and, when relevant, multiple scores or adjudication outcomes. Treating a noisy score as an exact ground truth can understate uncertainty even when the resampling code is flawless.

A useful report names the layers held fixed: dataset snapshot, question families, model versions, prompts, retrieval corpus, generation settings and scorer. It then states which variation the analysis actually explores. This is more precise than calling every interval a generic "confidence in the model."

Selection and assessment need separate evidence#

Prompt engineering, retrieval tuning, threshold choice and adapter selection all use observations to choose a system. If the final holdout is repeatedly inspected during that process, the selected configuration can overfit its cases even without updating model weights. The holdout has become development data for the overall system.

A clean workflow freezes the intended evaluation families and reserves a development partition for selection. After the policy is fixed, run the final assessment under that policy and retain all outcomes. If a severe failure leads to another change, be explicit that the previous assessment informed development and that a fresh assessment is needed for the next claim. This is not a punishment for learning from errors; it is a way to keep the evidence history interpretable.

Multiple comparisons create another selection effect. Trying many configurations and reporting only the best observed mean can select noise. The appropriate response depends on the study, but it always begins with reporting what was tried and which data supported the choice. A single final score cannot reveal that search history by itself.

Abstention turns one accuracy into a tradeoff#

Suppose system A answers all 10 requests and gets 8 correct. Its coverage is 1 and answered accuracy .8. System B answers 5 requests, all correctly, and abstains on 5. Its coverage is .5 and answered accuracy 1. Reporting only answered accuracy makes B look unambiguously better, while reporting only total answered-correct count favors A. The desired choice depends on the task's value and error costs.

Under an illustrative cost policy assigning 1 to a wrong answer,.1 to an abstention and 0 to a correct answer, A's average cost is 210=0.2\frac{2}{10}=0.2102​=0.2, while B's is(.1*5)/10=.05. But if the product also requires coverage at least .8, B fails that separate requirement. The cost values and coverage threshold are declared policy choices in this example, not universal constants. Preserve them before seeing which candidate benefits.

The all-abstain system has no answered-case accuracy because its answered denominator is zero. It should not receive a perfect score by convention. Report coverage 0 and an undefined conditional accuracy, then evaluate it under the stated utility and feasibility rules. This is another instance of refusing to turn missing observations into success.

Combine statistical summaries with required gates#

A mean difference and an interval can inform a decision, but some conditions may be required independently: no observed severe failures under the defined test, correct access boundaries, valid artifact identities or a minimum evidence coverage. A favorable average cannot logically imply those properties. Keep each required gate attached to the evidence that actually checked it.

Unknown is different from pass. A missing assessment should not be converted to True because other metrics look good. A no-go report can be complete if it identifies the failed or unknown condition and specifies what new evidence would allow reconsideration. This makes the decision useful for engineering rather than merely labeling the model good or bad.

Preserve a compact reliability argument#

The final packet should state the intended use, the paired comparison unit, family structure, weighting rule, active denominators, actual outcomes, uncertainty method and required gates. It should include enough raw paired results to reconstruct the summaries. The fixture's exact bootstrap enumeration is a model for this transparency: the reader can see what the resampling distribution contains and what it cannot establish.

Vocabulary and concepts#

Clustered resampling: Sampling related groups as units rather than individual rows.

Coverage: Fraction of questions receiving an answer.

Reliability argument: A decision supported by specified tests and bounded assumptions.

Syntax anatomy and reasoning sequence#

Align both systems by question ID, compute per-question loss or correctness differences, then average within family if families are the resampling unit. Use one index draw for the paired family differences. Preserve a decision ledger separating validation choices from final assessment. Calculate coverage=answered/total and selective accuracy=correct_answered/answered, with an explicit empty-answer policy.

The sampling unit determines the comparison#

The supplied question families are A, A, B, with paired score differences[-1, -1, +1]. An equal-row mean is-1/3. First averaging within family gives A=−1A=-1A=−1 and B=+1B=+1B=+1, whose equal-family mean is zero. These answer different questions: performance on the observed row mix versus performance when each sampled family receives equal weight. A report must say which target weighting it intends.

Now duplicate a negative A-type row ten more times. The equal-row mean becomes-11/13, but the two family means remain-1 and+1, so the equal-family mean remains zero. Duplicating related prompts increases the number of rows without necessarily increasing the number of independent units. This is why resampling arbitrary rows can understate uncertainty when the data have family structure.

With only these two empirical family means, drawing two families with replacement gives mean-1, 0 or+1 with probabilities 14\frac{1}{4}41​, 12\frac{1}{2}21​ and 14\frac{1}{4}41​. Running a thousand bootstrap draws samples that small empirical distribution more densely; it does not create a thousand new independent families or establish transfer to unrepresented families. The interval is conditional on the observed sample and chosen resampling scheme. More independent evaluation families would provide different evidence from more repetitions of the same bootstrap.

Accuracy and coverage describe different decisions#

If a system answers two of four questions and both answers are correct, coverage is 24=0.5\frac{2}{4}=0.542​=0.5 and selective accuracy is 22=1\frac{2}{2}=122​=1. A system answering none has zero coverage but undefined selective accuracy under this definition, not perfect accuracy. Correct abstention additionally needs answerability labels; a system could avoid errors simply by refusing questions it could have answered.

Keep prompt and threshold selection in the development process and reserve independent assessment data for the final declared system. Paired IDs make per-question changes visible, but do not by themselves remove selection bias. Required gates with unknown evidence should remain unknown and block release when the policy requires a pass. The confidence or average score in one column cannot silently substitute for missing provenance, severe-failure checks or coverage. This lesson checks the arithmetic and gate logic; it does not estimate real population reliability from three synthetic rows.

Worked example / walkthrough#

Example A: Align paired question results#

The repeated familyA receives twice the weight under a row mean. The family mean asks a different question and prevents variants from silently dominating the comparison.

import numpy as np

families=['A','A','B']; diff=np.array([-1.,-1.,1.]); grouped=np.array([diff[np.array(families)==f].mean() for f in ['A','B']])
np.testing.assert_array_equal(grouped,[-1,1]); np.testing.assert_allclose(diff.mean(),-1/3,atol=1e-12,rtol=0)

Example B: Resample whole families#

The sample space is tiny and visibly discrete. A smooth-looking interval would not create more independent families than the two observed.

import numpy as np

families=['A','A','B']; diff=np.array([-1.,-1.,1.]); grouped=np.array([diff[np.array(families)==f].mean() for f in ['A','B']])

Common pitfalls and diagnosis#

Choose the observational unit before averaging or bootstrapping. Duplicated family rows do not add independent families, and more bootstrap repetitions do not add new data. Report both answer coverage and conditional accuracy, preserve undefined denominators, and keep development selection separate from final assessment.
