## The question and the mechanism

Review a grouped cost rewrite under explicit assumptions before proposing a performance measurement.

A cost calculation can multiply each sales line's quantity by its product's constant unit cost, or aggregate quantities by product first and multiply once per product. These forms agree only when the grouping preserves every variable on which the rate depends. In our fixture UnitCost is explicitly constant per product across the modeled period.

The grouped expression still needs correct context transition for each product's quantity and cost. A filter on region or date must remain active while the product key changes. Unsold products yield blank quantities and should not create invented costs. Compare several contexts and edge cases rather than checking only the grand total.

This is a semantic-refactoring capstone, not a speed claim. The grouped iterator can have fewer logical rows but introduce other evaluation work; an engine may optimize either expression. If real costs vary by transaction date, supplier or currency, grouping only by ProductKey would be invalid. Preserve the required rate grain first, then design a qualified external measurement.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

The line form uses RELATED inside SUMX(Sales,...). The grouped form iterates ProductKey, transitions the key for the fact quantity, retrieves the unique product cost and multiplies. The two result columns are independent expression shapes over the same fixed assumptions.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **semantic parity**, **grouped rewrite**, **constant rate**, **cost model**, **qualification**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

### Case A: Whole-model parity under a constant product cost

**Starting context:** Clean retail model; UnitCost is constant per product and unique on ProductKey.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "LineCost", SUMX ( Sales, Sales[Quantity] * RELATED ( Product[UnitCost] ) ), "GroupedCost", SUMX ( VALUES ( Product[ProductKey] ), CALCULATE ( SUM ( Sales[Quantity] ) ) * CALCULATE ( MAX ( Product[UnitCost] ) ) ) )
```

**Context trace:**

1. Notebook has 7 units at 2, Pen 5 units at 1 and Mug 5 units at 5.
2. Grouped costs are 14,5 and 25, totaling 44.
3. The eight line-level costs independently sum to the same 44.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| LineCost | GroupedCost |
| --- | --- |
| 44 | 44 |

The data model supplies the rate constancy that permits this grouping. The formula alone does not prove that assumption in another dataset.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: Parity under a region and year selection

**Starting context:** North and order year 2025 remain active while the grouped expression iterates product keys.

```dax
EVALUATE CALCULATETABLE ( ROW ( "LineCost", SUMX ( Sales, Sales[Quantity] * RELATED ( Product[UnitCost] ) ), "GroupedCost", SUMX ( VALUES ( Product[ProductKey] ), CALCULATE ( SUM ( Sales[Quantity] ) ) * CALCULATE ( MAX ( Product[UnitCost] ) ) ) ), Customer[Region] = "North", 'Date'[Year] = 2025 )
```

**Context trace:**

1. S4,S5,S8 are the eligible lines.
2. Their costs are 6,4 and 10.
3. The grouped quantities 3,4,2 at rates 2,1,5 also yield 20.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| LineCost | GroupedCost |
| --- | --- |
| 20 | 20 |

This filtered case checks that a rewrite does not accidentally remove region or period constraints. Add missing-key and changing-rate cases before generalizing.

## Progressive hints

1. Notebook and Pen costs are 14 and 5.
2. Grouping only by ProductKey would combine different rates.
3. Separate proven fixture semantics from unmeasured speed.
4. An aggregate can hide a violated uniqueness contract.
5. Missing rates must not silently remove cost from a total.
6. The exchange rate depends ondate andcurrency, not product alone.
