## The question and the mechanism

Reconcile row values and a grand-total ratio without forcing the total to sum visible cells.

A total is another evaluation of the measure in a broader context. For additive revenue, that reevaluation often equals the sum of disjoint visible rows. For a ratio, distinct count, median or closing balance, it need not. A total that differs from the sum of row values is not automatically a broken total.

Paper has revenue 91 and 12 units, giving about 7.5833 USD per unit. Home has 96 and 5 units, giving 19.2. The whole population has revenue 187 and 17 units, giving 11. Adding the two row ratios produces about 26.7833, which has no sensible interpretation as the pooled price per unit.

If a business requirement truly asks for a sum or equal-weighted mean of displayed group results, express that separate measure with an explicit group iterator and label it accordingly. Do not overwrite a correct pooled ratio just to imitate a spreadsheet total. First identify the unit of each displayed number and the estimand at the total level.

## Vocabulary and expression anatomy

SUMMARIZECOLUMNS builds category groups and evaluates the named measures for each. Rows for which every supplied expression is BLANK are omitted, so unsold Travel does not appear here. The second query compares the pooled ratio with an explicitly summed category-ratio expression. The two outputs intentionally have different definitions.

Concepts to explain in your own words: **grand total**, **subtotal**, **nonadditive measure**, **ratio**, **reevaluation**.

## Model and context contract

The ordinary star follows Product → Sales, Customer → Sales and Date → Sales through OrderDate. The ShipDate relationship is inactive unless a case explicitly activates it. Filters flow from the one side to the many side. Additional analytical tables have only the relationships declared in their named model; a similar column name does not create a relationship.

```text
Product (one)  --> Sales (many) <-- Customer (one)
                       ^
                       | active OrderDate
                    Date (one)
                       | inactive ShipDate
                       +------> Sales
```

All amounts in ordinary Sales are USD after the line discount, before tax and returns. The Date fixture is complete for 2024 and 2025; a short result table is a selected slice, not the whole calendar. JSON `null` in the worked-results sidecar means DAX BLANK, not the text "null".

## Worked cases

The following query-local declarations are shared by these cases. They do not create persisted measures in this classroom.

```dax
DEFINE
    MEASURE Sales[NetRevenue] = SUM ( Sales[NetAmount] )
    MEASURE Sales[QuantitySold] = SUM ( Sales[Quantity] )
```

### Case A: Category-level prices

**Starting context:** One group per Category; no outer filters; all-blank Travel group omitted by this summary.

```dax
EVALUATE SUMMARIZECOLUMNS ( Product[Category], "Revenue", [NetRevenue], "Units", [QuantitySold], "NetPerUnit", DIVIDE ( [NetRevenue], [QuantitySold] ) )
ORDER BY Product[Category]
```

**Context trace:**

1. Home contributes 96 over 5 units.
2. Paper contributes 91 over 12 units.
3. Each ratio describes its own category population.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| Category | Revenue | Units | NetPerUnit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | 96 | 5 | 19.2 |
| Paper | 91 | 12 | 7.583333333 |

The two categories have different unit counts. An equal-weight operation over the ratios would change the weighting unit from sold units to categories.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

### Case B: The total and an intentionally different measure

**Starting context:** No category grouping for the pooled total; a separate iterator explicitly sums category ratios.

```dax
EVALUATE ROW ( "PooledNetPerUnit", DIVIDE ( [NetRevenue], [QuantitySold] ), "SumOfCategoryRatios", SUMX ( VALUES ( Product[Category] ), DIVIDE ( [NetRevenue], [QuantitySold] ) ) )
```

**Context trace:**

1. The pooled measure reevaluates 187/17 and returns 11.
2. The iterator evaluates 19.2 and 91/12 for the two nonblank categories.
3. Their sum is about 26.7833, a different defined calculation.

**Hand-derived worked-reference result:**

| PooledNetPerUnit | SumOfCategoryRatios |
| --- | --- |
| 11 | 26.78333333 |

The contrast is a useful review test: a total-row formula should reflect the intended definition, not merely resemble the visual's arithmetic layout.

Hand-derived for the named reference and fixture. Editing a draft does not recalculate this table.

## Progressive hints

1. Use their unit counts as weights.
2. There is now one contributing category.
3. Choose category weighting intentionally.
4. Customers can belong to several category populations.
5. All three expressions are blank for its empty fact population.
6. A balance is additive across products but generally not across dates.
