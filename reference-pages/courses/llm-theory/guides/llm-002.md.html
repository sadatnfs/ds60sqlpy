Purpose and mental model#

From relative scores to a choice distribution#

Suppose a model gives two next-token candidates scores 0 and log(3)\log(3). A score is not yet a probability: scores may be negative, may exceed one and need not sum to one. What matters initially is their relative preference. Softmax turns those scores into positive weights, then divides each weight by their total. The exponential of 0 is 1 and the exponential of log(3)\log(3) is 3, so the normalized probabilities are 14\frac{1}{4} and 34\frac{3}{4}. The second candidate receives three times as much probability as the first.

Let z_i denote the score of candidate i, with i ranging over the V allowed vocabulary entries. Write p_i for its resulting probability and Z for the sum of the exponential weights. Then Z=∑jezjZ=\sum_j e^{z_j} and pi=exp(zi)Zp_{i}=\frac{\exp(z_{i})}{Z}. The index j in the sum runs over candidate choices; it does not run over different questions or different token positions. For a batch of questions, each question needs its own denominator. This is why an axis argument is part of the meaning of the calculation, not a minor array detail.

The ratio pipj\frac{p_{i}}{p_{j}} equals exp(zi−zj)\exp(z_{i}-z_{j}). The shared denominator cancels, so subtracting the same number from every score leaves all probability ratios unchanged. That observation gives the stable implementation. For [1000,1000], computing exp(1000)\exp(1000) directly is unnecessary and may overflow. Subtract the maximum, producing [0,0], exponentiate to [1,1], then normalize to [0.5,0.5]. The equal probabilities are not an approximation introduced by the shift. They are exactly the distribution that the original equal scores describe, subject to ordinary floating-point rounding.

A mask changes the candidate set over which the denominator is defined. In the fixture [0,100,0] with only the first and third positions allowed, the middle score must contribute neither mass nor a stabilizing maximum. The valid calculation is therefore the two-way competition between equal scores, resulting in [0.5,0,0.5]. If the huge forbidden score is included first, it takes almost all the normalized mass. Deleting its probability afterward leaves a row that scarcely sums to anything. The error is a change in the probability experiment, not simply a wrong final display value.

Temperature changes score differences rather than shifting them. Dividing [0,log(3)][0,\log(3)] by two reduces the odds ratio from 3 to 3\sqrt{3}, spreading probability more evenly while preserving the ranking. A common offset preserves the distribution; a positive scale generally changes it. This difference will matter when we choose how to sample text. It also explains why two systems with the same most likely token can behave differently: one may put 0.51 on the winner, another 0.99.

Softmax is useful when exactly one candidate is to be selected from a defined set, as in next-token prediction or one attention row. It is not a reason to force mutually independent yes/no events into a single competition. Nor does normalization calibrate a model against reality. It guarantees a categorical distribution under its input scores and allowed set; factual or task reliability remains an empirical question.

Derive the normalizer before trusting the numbers#

For K candidates with finite scores z_1 through z_K, define Z=∑jezjZ=\sum_j e^{z_j} and pi=exp(zi)Zp_{i}=\frac{\exp(z_{i})}{Z}. The denominator is shared by every candidate in one decision. Increasing one score can therefore lower other candidates' probabilities even when their own scores do not change. A row of a matrix is one decision in this lesson. Summing over the entire matrix instead mixes decisions and changes the question being answered.

Subtracting the same constant c from every score preserves the distribution because ezi−c=ezie−ce^{z_i-c}=e^{z_i}e^{-c}; that common factor cancels from numerator and denominator. Choosing c=maxjzjc=\max_j z_j ensures that every exponential is at most one and that at least one is exactly one. This avoids unnecessary overflow in exponentiation and keeps the denominator positive. Very small relative masses can still underflow to zero. Numerical stability means avoiding avoidable representation failure, not representing every arbitrarily small probability exactly.

The two-candidate case exposes the meaning of a score difference. Algebra gives p1=11+exp(z2−z1)p_1=\frac{1}{1+\exp(z_2-z_1)} and p1p2=exp(z1−z2)\frac{p_1}{p_2}=\exp(z_1-z_2). Scores [log(3),0][\log(3),0] therefore yield [34,14][\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{4}]. Adding 1000 changes neither ratio. Dividing by temperature T changes the ratio to exp[(z_1-z_2)/T]. At T=2T=2 the same pair becomes [33+1,13+1][\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}+1},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}+1}], approximately [.634,.366]. The ranking is unchanged for positive T, while the separation shrinks. As T approaches zero from above, mass concentrates on maximal scores; a tie can retain multiple maxima. T=0T=0 is not a valid divisor in this formula.

A forbidden candidate must be excluded before the maximum and denominator are formed. With allowed scores [0,0] and a forbidden score 1000, the intended weights are [.5,.5,0]. Computing softmax over all three and setting the third result to zero afterward leaves the first two near zero, so the row no longer sums to one. Multiplying a forbidden score by zero before softmax is also wrong: a score of zero contributes exp(0)=1\exp(0)=1. The reference's negative-infinity mask removes forbidden mass, and its all-masked-row check prevents normalizing an empty choice set.

The Jacobian explains gradient coupling. Differentiate p_i with respect to z_j to obtain p_i(delta_ij-p_j), where delta_ij is 1 for i=ji=j and 0 otherwise. The diagonal p_i(1-p_i) is nonnegative; off-diagonal terms -p_i p_j are nonpositive. For p=[0.75,0.25]p=[0.75,0.25], the matrix is [[.1875,-.1875],[-.1875,.1875]]. Each row sums to zero because a common shift must have no effect. This identity is a useful independent check of a later backward implementation.

For one observed target y, loss L=−logL=-\mathrm{log} p_y has derivative dL/dz_j=p_j-1[j=y]. If candidate2 is the target in the [.75,.25] example, the gradient is [.75,-.75]. A small gradient-descent step lowers score 1 and raises score 2. A temperature inside the training softmax adds the factor 1T\frac{1}{T} to this derivative. That statement concerns this defined objective; changing a serving temperature after training does not run this gradient update or make the resulting probabilities calibrated.

Diagnose failures by inspecting the first broken invariant: finite scores, a nonempty allowed set, the intended key/candidate axis, finite shifted allowed scores, nonnegative mass, positive denominator and unit row sum. A unit sum alone cannot reveal a wrong axis or a swapped candidate identity. Retain a nonuniform example such as [log(3),0][\log(3),0], since equal scores cannot expose every scaling error. The probabilities describe choices under a scoring rule. Their numerical normalization is not evidence that the favored answer is true.

Interview reasoning: "Why not subtract the mean instead of the maximum?" Any common offset preserves the exact mathematical distribution. The maximum specifically prevents positive exponent arguments. A mean can leave an enormous positive score difference and therefore fail to prevent overflow. The choice of maximum is a numerical implementation decision supported by the cancellation identity.

Vocabulary and concepts#

Score: an unnormalized numeric preference; its magnitude alone is not a probability.

Softmax: exponentiation followed by normalization over a declared candidate axis.

Normalizer: the sum of allowed exponential weights that becomes the denominator.

Allowed mask: a boolean array specifying which choices may participate; True means included in this lesson.

Temperature: a positive score divisor that changes the concentration of softmax without changing the ranking of unequal finite scores.

Syntax or reasoning anatomy#

For one row, subtract the maximum allowed score, exponentiate the shifted values and divide by their sum. The subtraction changes every score by the same amount. That shared factor would cancel between numerator and denominator, but computing after subtraction avoids forming enormous exponentials. A forbidden choice receives zero exponential mass before normalization.

np.asarray converts input into a numeric array, while ndim and shape check the declared one-row interface. A boolean mask is distinct from integers whose truthiness happens to be nonzero. Reject an all-false row because no allowed categorical distribution exists. Keep axis-specific reasoning explicit: stacking two valid row distributions is different from normalizing all entries in a matrix as one choice.

Worked example / walkthrough#

Run Examples A and B in order in one scratch file, then run that complete file in a fresh process. Example B may reuse the imports, helpers and fixture state established in Example A. Do not treat an isolated later block as a standalone program.

Example A: A stable shift of equal scores#

Implement the basic formula on two finite fixtures. No random draw is necessary.

import numpy as np

for scores in ([0.0, 0.0], [1000.0, 1000.0]):
    values = np.asarray(scores, dtype=np.float64)
    mass = np.exp(values - values.max())
    probabilities = mass / mass.sum()
    np.testing.assert_allclose(probabilities, [0.5, 0.5], rtol=0, atol=1e-12)
    print(probabilities)

Expected: Both rows print the same two halves. The large offset has no effect on relative probabilities, and the shifted exponentials never require exp(1000)\exp(1000).

Example B: Forbidden mass must not reach the denominator#

Keep a huge score in a forbidden middle choice; remove it before normalization.

scores = np.array([0.0, 100.0, 0.0])
allowed = np.array([True, False, True])
masked = np.where(allowed, scores, -np.inf)
mass = np.exp(masked - masked.max())
probabilities = mass / mass.sum()
np.testing.assert_allclose(probabilities, [0.5, 0.0, 0.5], rtol=0, atol=1e-12)
print(probabilities)

Expected: The forbidden high score has no effect on the two legal alternatives. An all-false mask would make the row invalid and must be rejected before this formula.

Progressive hints#

  • Hint 1: Start with equal legal scores so their normalized weights must be equal.
  • Hint 2: Subtract the maximum over allowed scores before exponentiation.
  • Hint 3: Check the row sum after masking, and reject a row whose legal set is empty.

Common pitfalls#

If a probability is NaN, inspect empty or fully forbidden choices before adding small constants to a denominator. If scores are large, inspect whether exponentiation happened before maximum subtraction. If every value looks plausible but row sums are one half, inspect the normalization axis. Nonnegativity alone does not define a valid categorical row.

A boolean mask can have opposite meanings in different libraries. Translate the inclusion rule instead of trusting the variable name. Temperature zero is not passed into this division formula; a future decoder can implement a separate greedy branch. A normalized weight is a model-dependent preference, not independent evidence that its corresponding answer is factually correct.
