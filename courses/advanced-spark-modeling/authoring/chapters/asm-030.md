## Starting point and result

A large training job spends time reading, converting and moving data as well as differentiating a model. The integration must define what an epoch means and how a restart resumes it. This chapter distinguishes Spark partitions, persistent training shards and Torch ranks, then designs evidence for a complete data-delivery path.

## Mechanism: identities survive execution rearrangement

A durable shard should identify an immutable dataset version, schema, feature order, observation-ID range or index, row count and checksum. A rank is a process role in one training run. A Spark partition is an execution subdivision that can change after repartitioning. Neither rank number nor physical partition number alone is a durable data identity.

An epoch may mean one pass over all declared observations, a fixed number of sampled steps or a weighted resampling schedule. State that definition. For exact once-per-epoch coverage, the union of processed IDs must equal the dataset and assignments must have no duplicates. For resampling, record the sampling distribution and seed rather than pretending the epoch is a unique pass.

A restart checkpoint may need model weights, optimizer moments, scheduler state, random-number-generator state, epoch and sampler position. Resuming only weights changes the subsequent trajectory for optimizers with state. Changing world size can also change batch composition and numerical reduction, so an “exact resume” claim needs a narrower contract than “the job starts again.”

## Worked case: five IDs and two ranks

Use IDs zero through four. Compare three delivery policies: padding to six assignments, dropping to four, and an explicit uneven five-ID assignment. The first duplicates at least one ID; the second omits one; the third needs a collective schedule that handles unequal work. The project's `sharding-audit` case should report all three outcomes before any learning-rate interpretation.

The correct choice depends on the objective. Approximate stochastic training may accept padding, while evaluating an exact held-out likelihood should avoid accidental duplication. A reported average must divide by the actual intended weight, not merely the number of loader iterations.

## Worked case: checkpoint between epochs

A bounded reproducibility test trains for a fixed number of steps, saves complete state at an epoch boundary, restarts in the same prepared environment and compares with an uninterrupted reference. The expected comparison includes parameters, optimizer state, step count and ID schedule. If only final loss is close, describe it as numerical agreement rather than bitwise trajectory equivalence.

Checkpointing mid-batch or during an outstanding collective is more complex. A production system needs an atomic artifact protocol and a definition of whether partially completed work is replayed. The teaching project does not infer such guarantees from a temporary file existing on one rank.

## Failure diagnosis

Low accelerator utilization may indicate input conversion, small batches or remote storage latency. A repeated first batch suggests the iterator or sampler is recreated incorrectly. Different results after restart may follow from missing optimizer state, altered shuffle seeds or a changed world size. Inspect those causes before blaming nondeterministic kernels.

## Interview discussion

**Should Spark feed tensors directly or materialize shards?** Both can be reasonable. Direct delivery can reduce storage steps; durable shards simplify reuse, audit and independent training retries. Measure conversion and storage costs and consider the required recovery contract.

**Is every restart supposed to be bitwise identical?** No. Declare whether the goal is exact trajectory, bounded numerical equivalence or statistical reproducibility. Preserve sufficient evidence for the chosen claim.
