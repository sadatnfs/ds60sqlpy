# js-050: Scatterplots, size encodings and overplotting

## Learning objectives

Build a three-variable scatterplot. Explain a correlation without claiming causation. Produce a chart whose data transformation, visual encoding and interaction can be explained and checked independently.

## Purpose, vocabulary and mental model

A scatterplot uses two quantitative positions to show the joint distribution of observations. Each point should have a defined row grain, such as one person or one experiment. A visible association does not by itself establish causality, and a few synthetic points cannot support a population conclusion.

When a third quantity controls circle size, decide whether the quantity maps to radius or area. Viewers perceive the filled area, so a square-root radius scale often makes area proportional to a nonnegative quantity. Doubling a radius quadruples area. A legend should explain the size meaning.

Overplotting occurs when points overlap. Opacity, smaller marks, aggregation or a density representation can help, but each changes what the reader can infer. Jitter can reveal repeated values while deliberately moving positions; explain and bound it rather than presenting moved points as exact measurements.

## Syntax anatomy and mechanism

Use independent x and y scales for their units. A `scaleSqrt` radius range maps nonnegative magnitude to radius. A circle at `(x(d.x), y(d.y))` encodes one record. Colors can represent categories, but labels or shapes should support readers who cannot distinguish the colors.

The reference validates finite positions and the area relationship for magnitudes 1 and 4. It does not perform a statistical test. Use a tooltip or table for exact records and make missing or invalid coordinates an explicit data policy.

## Worked example / walkthrough

### Worked example 1: Compare radius and area

The ratio follows the square of radius.

```javascript
const r = d3.scaleSqrt().domain([0, 4]).range([0, 20]);
console.log(r(1), r(4));
```

**Expected observation:** 10 and 20; areas have ratio 1 to 4.

### Worked example 2: Detect duplicate coordinates

Repeated positions can hide observations.

```javascript
const points = [
  { x: 1, y: 2 },
  { x: 1, y: 2 },
  { x: 2, y: 3 },
];
console.log(new Set(points.map((d) => `${d.x},${d.y}`)).size);
```

**Expected observation:** Two unique positions for three records.

## Debugging and common mistakes

Keep three checkpoints separate: the records after parsing, the domain and geometry after transformation, and the visible marks after rendering. A chart can look plausible while silently dropping records or assigning the wrong scale. Inspect counts, IDs, finite coordinates and meaningful expected values before adjusting colors. A missing mark may come from invalid input, a missing selection, an undefined scale result or clipping outside the viewBox.

The controlled failure for this lesson is: Diagnose mapping magnitude directly to radius. Its distinguishing evidence is Area exaggerates large values quadratically. Preserve that failing case when repairing your program. Do not remove an assertion or broaden a scale merely to hide a wrong input. Explain whether a changed value should affect the data, an encoding, a layout or only presentation.

## Accessibility and interpretation

Give the visualization a descriptive title and a short textual summary of the main result. Keep a table or list when exact values matter. Do not encode a critical category only through color; use labels, shapes or position as appropriate. Interactive controls need keyboard operation and clear focus, and motion should respect the user's reduced-motion preference. Automated DOM checks support, but do not replace, a real keyboard and assistive-technology review.

A data scientist must also defend the statistical meaning. State the population, units, aggregation, missing-value policy and uncertainty where relevant. Synthetic points illustrate a mechanism and do not establish a real-world finding. A beautiful visualization should make a valid claim easier to inspect, not make a weak claim appear certain.
